# 03 · Cookies, bars and brownies

> **Private reference. Not for publication.** Chef and outlet names stay in this file only. All sources were opened on 22 Oct 2026 and are on `SOURCES-LOCKED.md`.
> Always weigh flour and sugar (King Arthur: under-measured flour is a main cause of flat cookies). The ratios below are **% of flour weight** (baker's %).

## K1. Reference ratios (drop cookies, chocolate chip type)
Worked out from three tested recipes:

| Recipe | Butter % of flour | Total sugar % of flour | Brown : white | Egg | Salt % of flour | Oven |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food Lab (Serious Eats / Kenji) | 80 % (browned) | 100 % | 1 : 1 | 100 g per 280 g flour | ≈ 1.4 % | 160 °C / 325 °F |
| Jacques Torres (NYT Cooking) | 59 % (creamed) | 106 % | 1.25 : 1 | 2 eggs per 482 g flour | coarse salt + flaky | 175 °C / 350 °F |
| Brown-butter cookies (NYT Cooking) | 71 % (browned) | 84 % | 4.4 : 1 | 1 egg + 1 yolk per 320 g | ≈ 1.9 % (fine sea salt) | 175 °C / 350 °F |

- **Rule:** for a balanced chewy-crisp cookie, use **butter 60–80 % of flour weight**, **sugar 85–105 %** and **salt 1.2–2 %** (about 0.4–0.6 % of total dough). Use **baking soda about 1 %** of the flour when there is brown sugar. A recipe outside these ranges must explain why, for example a thin crisp cookie with more sugar.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "The Food Lab's Chocolate Chip Cookies" https://www.seriouseats.com/the-food-lab-best-chocolate-chip-cookie-recipe · NYT Cooking, "Chocolate Chip Cookies" (adapted from Jacques Torres) https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1015819-chocolate-chip-cookies · NYT Cooking, "Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies" https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1024108-brown-butter-chocolate-chip-cookies · King Arthur, "Why did my cookies come out flat?" (weigh flour) https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/08/26/why-did-my-cookies-come-out-flat

## K2. Creaming (softened butter + sugar)
- **Rule:** butter should be **cool room temperature (about 18 °C / 65 °F)**. It should dent with one finger but still feel slightly cool, never greasy. Beat with the sugar at **medium speed (stand mixer 3–4) for 2–3 min**, scraping halfway, until **pale and fluffy** (pale yellow, not white). Then add the eggs one at a time.
  - Stella Parks creams 5 min for maximum aeration in sugar cookies. The Torres recipe also uses 5 min. Dorie Greenspan creams World Peace Cookies about 3 min, to "soft, creamy and homogenous".
- **Why:** sugar crystals cut air pockets into the plastic butter. The trapped air expands in the oven and gives lift and a lighter, more tender crumb. Stella's creamed dough made 26 one-ounce cookies against 21 for unaerated dough. Butter that is too cold can't trap air. Butter that is too warm or melted lets the air collapse.
- **Failure → fix:** cookies that are **flat and spread**, which King Arthur names as the most common cause, come from **over-creaming** (the butter falls out of emulsion) or warm butter. Cream 2–3 min only and chill the dough. A dense, grainy mix means the butter was too cold.
- **Disagreement:** creaming time ranges from 2–3 min (King Arthur, with modern mixers) to 5 min (Serious Eats, Torres). **Pick:** 2–3 min as the default, unless a recipe deliberately wants a cakey or puffy cookie.
- **Sources:** King Arthur, "Creaming butter and sugar: How to get it right" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2015/04/27/creaming-butter-sugar · Serious Eats / Stella Parks, "Cookie Science: Why Cream Butter and Sugar?" https://www.seriouseats.com/cookie-science-creaming-butter-sugar · King Arthur, "Why did my cookies come out flat?" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/08/26/why-did-my-cookies-come-out-flat · Dorie Greenspan, "World Peace Cookies" https://doriegreenspan.com/recipe/world-peace-cookies-the-newest-version-from-dories-cookies-sneak-peek/

## K3. Melted or browned butter (for chew)
- **Rule:** melt the butter (or brown it), cool it, then **stir by hand** with the sugars and eggs. **Brown butter:** melt over medium heat, swirling, until the milk solids are **golden-brown and nutty, about 5 min**. Pour it out at once, because it burns fast. **Browning removes water (≈ 15 % of butter).** Make up some of it (Food Lab whisks in **1 ice cube ≈ 30 ml per 225 g butter**) or expect a softer, more tender cookie. Cool the browned butter until it starts to turn opaque before mixing.
- **Why:** melted butter frees its water to meet the flour, which develops gluten and gives chew. Less whipped-in air makes a denser cookie. Browned milk solids add toffee and nut flavour.
- **Cue:** the butter foams, then goes quiet. The solids on the bottom turn amber-brown and it smells of hazelnut.
- **Failure → fix:** cakey cookies come from too much air, so stir by hand rather than whipping. Greasy cookies come from hot butter, so cool it first. Black specks and a bitter taste mean the butter burned, so pour it out of the hot pan the moment it browns.
- **Sources:** ATK, "How to Make the Chewiest Cookies and Bars" (melt or brown; hand-stir; brown sugar keeps chew) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7676-how-to-make-chewy-cookies · Serious Eats, "The Food Lab's Chocolate Chip Cookies" (brown butter, ice cube, butter is ≈ 15 % water) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-food-lab-best-chocolate-chip-cookie-recipe · NYT Cooking, "Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies" (brown ~5 min, mix by hand) https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1024108-brown-butter-chocolate-chip-cookies

## K4. Sugar: white vs brown, and texture
- **Rule:** **more white sugar → thinner, crisper, more spread. More brown sugar → softer, moister, chewier.** Brown sugar also reacts with baking soda (it is acidic) to give lift. Extra white sugar (+2 tbsp) with 2–4 extra minutes of baking turns a cookie crisp (King Arthur). A small amount of corn syrup (about 10 % of the sugar) makes cookies very flexible and chewy (Food Lab).
- **Why:** brown sugar's molasses brings invert sugars, which are hygroscopic. They hold water and stop sugar recrystallising, so the cookie stays bendy. White sugar recrystallises as it cools, which makes it crisp, and with a neutral pH it lets the dough spread before it sets. In creamed doughs, white sugar aerates better than brown.
- **Caution:** the effect depends on the method. Stella Parks shows that brown sugar can make cookies spread **more** in a creamed dough with no soda, and white sugar can keep sugar cookies thick. Always judge sugar together with the fat method and the leavening.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats / Stella Parks, "Cookie Science: The Real Differences Between Brown and White Sugars" https://www.seriouseats.com/faq-difference-brown-white-granulated-sugar-baking-cookies · King Arthur, "Cookie science: How to achieve your perfect chocolate chip cookie" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2016/12/21/cookie-science · Serious Eats, "The Food Lab's Chocolate Chip Cookies" (Cookie Fact #8; corn syrup) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-food-lab-best-chocolate-chip-cookie-recipe · ATK, "How to Make the Chewiest Cookies and Bars" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7676-how-to-make-chewy-cookies

## K5. Resting or chilling the dough
- **Rule:** chill scooped dough for **at least 1 h** for shape control. **Rest it 24–72 h** for the deepest flavour (Torres 24–36 h, up to 72; Food Lab overnight up to 3 days). Cover the surface tightly. Slice-and-bake logs keep up to 3 days refrigerated or 2 months frozen. Bake frozen logs straight from the freezer, adding about 1 min (Dorie).
- **Why:** the flour hydrates fully, which gives a firmer dough that spreads less. Enzymes break starch and protein into sugars and amino acids, so the cookies brown more and taste of butterscotch. Cold butter melts later, so the edges set before the cookie spreads.
- **Disagreement:** the minimum chill ranges from 30 min (King Arthur), 1 h (NYT brown-butter recipe) and overnight (Food Lab) to 24 h (Torres). **Pick:** recipes give **"chill at least 1 h; best 24 h"** unless they are tested for no rest.
- **Failure → fix:** cookies that are pale and flat with a one-note taste came from unrested dough, so rest it overnight.
- **Sources:** NYT Cooking, "Chocolate Chip Cookies" (Torres; 24–36 h, up to 72 h) https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1015819-chocolate-chip-cookies · Serious Eats, "The Food Lab's Chocolate Chip Cookies" (Cookie Fact #20: overnight rest) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-food-lab-best-chocolate-chip-cookie-recipe · King Arthur, "Why did my cookies come out flat?" (chill 30 min–1 h or overnight) https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/08/26/why-did-my-cookies-come-out-flat · NYT Cooking, "Brown Butter Chocolate Chip Cookies" (1–24 h) https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1024108-brown-butter-chocolate-chip-cookies · Dorie Greenspan, "World Peace Cookies" https://doriegreenspan.com/recipe/world-peace-cookies-the-newest-version-from-dories-cookies-sneak-peek/

## K6. Baking and doneness cues (chewy cookies)
- **Rule:** bake at **160–180 °C (325–350 °F)** on parchment-lined **metal** sheets (King Arthur found foil-lined sheets made thin, flat cookies). Rotate the sheets halfway. **Pull them when the edges are golden and set but the centres still look soft, even slightly underdone.** Cool them **on the sheet for 2–10 min**, then move them to a rack.
  - Times: a 30 g (1 oz) ball takes **13–16 min at 160 °C** (Food Lab). A 60 g (¼ cup) ball takes **about 15 min at 175 °C** (NYT). A 100 g (3½ oz) ball takes **18–20 min at 175 °C** (Torres). Slice-and-bake World Peace Cookies take **12 min at 160 °C**, and "won't look done" (Dorie).
- **Why:** carryover heat on the hot sheet finishes the centre. Cookies baked until they look done harden into dry, cakey or crunchy cookies once cool. A cool oven lets the dough spread before it sets, so always check with an oven thermometer.
- **Optional:** bang the pan on the counter as it comes out for a flatter, chewier cookie with ripples (NYT brown-butter recipe).
- **Failure → fix:** hard, dry cookies were baked to "done-looking", so pull them 1–2 min earlier. If they are raw in the middle after cooling, the balls are bigger than the timing assumes, so weigh the portions.
- **Sources:** ATK, "Knowing When Cookies and Brownies Are Done" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/5556-knowing-when-cookies-and-brownies-are-done · Serious Eats, "The Food Lab's Chocolate Chip Cookies" https://www.seriouseats.com/the-food-lab-best-chocolate-chip-cookie-recipe · NYT Cooking, "Chocolate Chip Cookies" (Torres) https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1015819-chocolate-chip-cookies · Dorie Greenspan, "World Peace Cookies" https://doriegreenspan.com/recipe/world-peace-cookies-the-newest-version-from-dories-cookies-sneak-peek/ · King Arthur, "Why did my cookies come out flat?" (foil sheets; oven too cool) https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/08/26/why-did-my-cookies-come-out-flat

## K7. Crisp vs chewy vs cakey: quick levers
| Want | Change | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Crisper | +white sugar, bake 2–4 min longer, more spread | King Arthur cookie science |
| Chewier | melted or browned butter, more brown sugar, hand-stir, underbake slightly, some oil with the butter | ATK chewy; Food Lab |
| Cakier or puffier | creamed butter (longer), less sugar, a little milk (King Arthur: 2 tbsp) | King Arthur; Serious Eats creaming |
| Thicker | chill the dough, weigh the flour, don't over-cream, cold dough onto a cool sheet | King Arthur flat cookies |

## K8. Brownies (fudgy, glossy, crackly top)
- **Rule (fudgy, Stella Parks method):**
  1. Brown the butter until golden and **silent**, then stir in the chopped dark chocolate.
  2. **Whip the eggs + sugars + salt on medium-high until very thick and fluffy (about 8 min).** This makes the shiny, crackly top.
  3. Stream in the warm chocolate-butter, then add the sifted flour and cocoa.
  4. Bake in a **light-coloured metal (anodized aluminium) 23 × 33 cm pan** lined with a foil sling, at **180 °C / 350 °F for about 30 min** to an internal **96 °C (205 °F)**. Insert the probe at an angle and don't touch the pan.
  5. **Cool completely** before cutting.
- **Doneness (ATK):** a toothpick in the centre comes out with **a few moist crumbs**. A clean toothpick means overbaked.
- **Pan:** **glass dishes give noticeably drier brownies** (glass holds heat, so they keep baking). If a recipe uses glass, shorten the time and say so.
- **Chew lever (ATK):** use **butter + vegetable oil in about a 1 : 3 ratio** (saturated + unsaturated fat) for chewy brownies. Brown sugar or corn syrup gives a matte top. White sugar gives a shiny one.
- **Failure → fix:** dry, cakey or chalky brownies were overbaked or baked in glass, so pull them at moist crumbs or 96 °C. If there is no crackly top, the eggs and sugar weren't whipped enough. If they are gummy and collapsed, they were cut warm or underbaked, so cool fully.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats / Stella Parks, "Glossy Fudge Brownies" https://www.seriouseats.com/bravetart-glossy-fudge-brownies · ATK, "A Guide to Making Brownies" (toothpick; glass vs metal; 1 : 3 butter-to-oil; sugar and shine) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/665-a-guide-to-making-brownies · ATK, "Knowing When Cookies and Brownies Are Done" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/5556-knowing-when-cookies-and-brownies-are-done

## K9. Bars (blondies and other bar cookies) — expanded in pass 2
- **Rule (blondies):** **melted butter** (browned for a nuttier bar), **light brown sugar**, hand-stirred. Base about **1 : 1 sugar to flour**. Season boldly: about 1 tsp salt and up to 2 tbsp vanilla per 13×9 pan, flaky salt on top. Use a **foil sling**. Bake at **175 °C / 350 °F**: a 13×9 pan takes **22–25 min**, until the top is shiny, cracked and light golden. **Do not overbake.** Cool fully in the pan before cutting.
- **Reference formula (ATK Congo bars, 13×9):** 213 g flour, 1 tsp baking powder, ½ tsp salt, 170 g melted butter, 298 g light brown sugar, 2 eggs, 4 tsp vanilla, plus chocolate, toasted coconut and nuts.
- **Why:** melting the butter instead of creaming it gives a dense, chewy bar instead of a cakey one. Brown sugar's moisture gives chew and a butterscotch flavour. ATK found that cutting the sugar made the bars drier and tougher. To make them less sweet, ATK swapped part of the brown sugar for corn syrup rather than cutting it. Brownie batter made without chocolate came out greasy and gummy, because it lacks the cocoa starch. Cookie dough pressed into a pan came out dry and tough. So blondies need their own formula.
- **Cue:** shiny, crackled, light-golden top, with edges just set. A toothpick inserted **5 cm / 2 in from the side** should come out **almost clean** (Milk Bar).
- **Failure → fix:**
  - Cakey → melted butter, not creamed; mix by hand.
  - Greasy or gummy → don't adapt a brownie recipe; use a blondie formula.
  - Dry or tough → you overbaked it or cut the sugar; pull it at the shiny-top stage.
- **Own judgment (marked):** for an 8×8 pan, use half the 13×9 formula. Start checking at 20 min and judge by the toothpick cue. Milk Bar's mix-based 8×8 blondie bakes at 325 °F for 25–30 min. Its formula is a packaged mix, so only the cue and timing are used, not the ratios. Keeps about **1 week** covered at room temperature (ATK and Milk Bar agree).
- **Sources:**
  - ATK, "How to Make the Perfect Blondie": https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/1194-how-to-make-the-perfect-blondie
  - ATK, "Congo Bars": https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/1837-congo-bars
  - Milk Bar (Tosi), "Chocolate Chunk Blondies": https://milkbarstore.com/blogs/recipes/chocolate-chunk-blondies
  - ATK, chewy cookies and bars: https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7676-how-to-make-chewy-cookies

## K10. Safety for cookie dough and batters
- **Rule:** recipes must not invite people to eat raw dough or batter (raw egg and raw flour). Bake fully, to the cues above. FSIS lists egg dishes at 71 °C / 160 °F. Brownies at 96 °C are far above that.
- **Sources:** FSIS chart https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart · FoodSafety.gov chart https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures

## K11. Shortbread
- **Rule:** use a ratio of **butter : sugar : starch ≈ 2 : 1 : 2–3** by weight, with **no egg and no leavener**. Part of the flour can be cornflour or semolina for a short, sandy crumb.
  - Paul Hollywood: 225 g butter / 110 g caster sugar / 225 g plain flour + 110 g cornflour.
  - Mary Berry: 225 g butter / 100 g caster sugar / 225 g flour + 100 g semolina.
  - King Arthur: 227 g butter / 113 g confectioners' sugar / 240 g flour.
  - Add a pinch to ¼ tsp salt (½ tsp if the butter is unsalted).
  - Dock (prick) the dough and chill it until firm (at least 30 min).
  - Bake **low**:

    | Source | Shape | Oven | Time |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Paul Hollywood | rolled 1 cm | 170 °C | about 20 min |
    | Mary Berry | pressed in a tin | 160 °C / 140 °C fan | about 35 min |
    | King Arthur | pressed in a pan | 325 °F (163 °C) | 35–40 min |
- **Why:** with no egg or water, little gluten forms, which gives the "short" texture. Cornflour or semolina dilutes the gluten further and adds crunch. A low oven sets the dough without browning, and docking stops it puffing or bubbling.
- **Cue:** **pale to just golden** all over, a little deeper at the edges. Never brown. Cut pan shortbread into fingers or wedges **while still warm** (5–10 min out of the oven). It snaps cleanly once cold.
- **Failure → fix:**
  - Greasy or tough → too much handling or re-rolling; re-roll scraps **once** only (Paul Hollywood).
  - Spread or blurred edges → chill before baking.
  - Crumbles when cut → cut while warm.
  - Too brown → lower the oven.
- **Keeps:** 3–4 days airtight (Hollywood).
- **Sources:**
  - Paul Hollywood, "Buttery Shortbread": https://www.paulhollywood.com/post/buttery-shortbread
  - King Arthur, "Shortbread Cookies": https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/shortbread-cookies-recipe
  - Mary Berry, "The Very Best Shortbread" (excerpt from her book on the publisher's Happy Foodie site): https://thehappyfoodie.co.uk/recipes/the-very-best-shortbread/

## K12. Sugar cookies (cut-out, no-spread)
- **Rule:**
  - Use an **unleavened or lightly leavened** dough of creamed butter (or part shortening), sugar, 1 egg and flour. Sugar Geek uses equal weights of butter and sugar to about 2.25× flour (8 oz : 8 oz : 18 oz). King Arthur adds 56 g cornstarch to 300 g flour.
  - Chill the dough (30 min KA; 2 h Sugar Geek). Roll **between parchment** to **6 mm / ¼ in** for sturdy cookies or **3 mm / ⅛ in** for delicate ones.
  - **Chill the cut-outs about 15 min**, then bake at **175 °C / 350 °F**: about 10 min for ⅛ in, 10–14 min for ¼ in.
- **Why:**
  - Leaveners and warm fat cause spread. Shortening melts at a higher temperature than butter, so it holds edges better.
  - Rolling between parchment avoids working in extra flour, which toughens the dough.
  - Cutting sugar by about 10 % or chilling for 30–60 min also reduces spread (KA).
- **Cue:** edges **very lightly golden**, centres pale and set. Cookies should look matte, not wet.
- **Failure → fix:**
  - Spreads → chill longer, switch part of the butter to shortening, and check that flour was weighed.
  - Tough → too much bench flour; re-roll less.
  - Dough splits or curdles → have butter and egg at room temperature (Sugar Geek).
- **Sources:**
  - KA, "Cut-out sugar cookies" tips: https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2016/12/11/cut-out-sugar-cookies
  - KA, "Classic Cut-Out Cookies": https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/classic-cut-out-cookies-recipe
  - KA, "Why are my cookies spreading?": https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/12/19/why-are-my-cookies-spreading

## K13. Macarons
- **Rule:** use equal weights of **almond flour and icing sugar**, sifted together. Two meringue methods:
  - **French (home default):** 75 g whites + 75 g sugar + ¼ tsp cream of tartar, with 85 g almond flour + 100 g icing sugar (KA). Le Cordon Bleu uses 65 g almond + 110 g icing sugar with 60 g whites + 27 g sugar.
  - **Italian (Hermé):** 80 g almond + 80 g icing sugar + 30 g whites makes the paste. A syrup of 80 g sugar + 25 g water is cooked to **118 °C** and poured onto another 30 g whites, then whipped until cool. The Italian method is more stable.
- **Whipping:** whip French meringue to **medium-firm, glossy peaks**: KA says "soft-serve tip", Le Cordon Bleu says stiff peaks. Do not whip it dry.
- **Macaronage:** fold, then smear the batter up the side of the bowl until it **falls in a thick continuous ribbon that slowly sinks back** and the surface is shiny.
- **Pipe and rest:** pipe 3.5–4 cm rounds (8 mm nozzle), rap the tray, then rest uncovered **15 min–2 h until a skin forms** (matte and not sticky to a light touch).
- **Bake low:**

  | Source | Oven | Time |
  |---|---|---|
  | Le Cordon Bleu | 140 °C | about 12 min |
  | Hermé | 150 °C | about 13 min, turning the tray at about 9 min |
  | KA | 160 °C (320–325 °F) | 15–20 min (drop to 150 °C / 300 °F if colouring) |

  **House:** 150 °C, about 13–15 min, oven-tested.
- **Cool and mature:** cool on the tray. Fill, then **refrigerate filled shells 24–48 h** to mature (KA; Hermé matures overnight). Serve at room temperature after about 1 h out of the fridge.
- **Why:** the skin forces steam out through the base, which forms the "foot" instead of a cracked top. Macaronage knocks out large bubbles but keeps small ones. Maturing lets moisture from the filling soften the shell into the classic chewy interior.
- **Cue:** **done when the shell lifts cleanly off the mat** and doesn't wobble on its foot (Hermé, KA).
- **Failure → fix:**
  - Cracked tops, no feet → under-rested, or the batter is too stiff.
  - Flat, spread, no feet → over-mixed batter.
  - Hollow → under-mixed or oven too hot.
  - Browned → lower the oven or put a tray above.
  - Sticking → bake longer; freeze the tray for 15–20 min to release.
  - Dull batter → add a little egg white (Le Cordon Bleu).
- **Storage:** about 1 week chilled, 1 month frozen (KA).
- **Safety:** the shells are baked. Italian meringue reaches 118 °C syrup. Fillings follow 05-CK12 and CK13.

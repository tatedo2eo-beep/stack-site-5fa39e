## CK1. Base ratios (butter cakes)
- **Rule:**
  - **Classic pound / Victoria sponge:** **equal weights of fat, sugar, flour and egg**. Mary Berry uses 225 g each of fat, caster sugar and self-raising flour + 4 large eggs (about 225 g) + 1 tsp baking powder, for two 20 cm tins.
  - **Modern pound cakes** often use **slightly less flour** than the other three, for a moister crumb (King Arthur).
- **Checks for a recipe:**
  - Flour more than about 110 % of the egg weight in a butter cake points to dryness.
  - Self-raising flour needs **no more than about 1 tsp extra baking powder** per 225 g.
  - **Fill pans no more than ⅔ full.** Tube pans can be filled to within about 2.5 cm of the top.
- **Why:** flour and egg give structure, while sugar and fat tenderise. Too much flour makes a cake dense, dry and bland.
- **Sources:** Mary Berry, "Victoria Sponge Sandwich" https://maryberry.co.uk/recipes/recipe/victoria-sponge-sandwich · King Arthur, "How to make the ultimate pound cake" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2020/08/17/how-to-make-the-ultimate-pound-cake · Beranbaum, "Rose's Tips for Cake Baking" (⅔ full) https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2009/11/16/roses_tips_for_cake_baking

## CK2. Ingredient temperature and prep
- **Rule:**
  - **Butter:** "cool but squishable", **18–24 °C (65–75 °F)**. It should take a fingertip dent with some resistance and not be greasy.
  - **Eggs and dairy at room temperature.** Warm cold eggs in hot tap water for about 5 min.
  - **Preheat the oven at least 20 min.** Bake in the centre of the oven, with pans at least 2.5 cm apart.
  - **Dark pans or fan oven: lower the temperature by about 15 °C (25 °F).**
  - Line pan bases with parchment.
- **Why:** cold butter won't hold air, and butter that is too warm collapses the air cells. Cold eggs can split a creamed batter.
- **Failure → fix:** if the batter curdles or splits on adding the eggs, they were too cold or added too fast. Add them one at a time at room temperature.
- **Sources:** Beranbaum, "My Method for Mixing Layer Cakes" https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2005/12/17/my_method_for_mixing_layer_cak · Beranbaum, "Rose's Tips for Cake Baking" https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2009/11/16/roses_tips_for_cake_baking · King Arthur, "Creaming butter and sugar" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2015/04/27/creaming-butter-sugar · King Arthur, "Ultimate pound cake" (warming eggs) https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2020/08/17/how-to-make-the-ultimate-pound-cake

## CK3. Creaming method
- **Rule (sequence):**
  1. Beat the butter and sugar at **medium (stand mixer speed 3–4) for 2–3 min**, scraping halfway, until **pale yellow (not white) and fluffy**, with sugar barely gritty between your fingers.
  2. Beat in the eggs **one at a time**.
  3. Add the dry and wet ingredients alternately (start and end with dry) on low, **just until combined**.
  - **All-in-one variant (Mary Berry):** everything in one bowl, beaten about 2 min until well blended.
- **Why:** sugar crystals cut air pockets into plastic butter. The leavener's gas inflates these, which gives lift and a fine crumb. Over-creaming (near-white) gives dense, gummy streaks.
- **Cue:** a domed cake with a fluffier, more open crumb than a reverse-creamed one.
- **Failure → fix:** a dense, heavy cake means the butter was too cold or under-creamed. A greasy, sunken cake means the butter was too warm or melted. Tunnels and toughness come from overmixing after the flour.
- **Sources:** King Arthur, "Creaming butter and sugar" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2015/04/27/creaming-butter-sugar · ATK, "How to Reverse Cream" (creaming explained) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7575-how-to-reverse-cream · Mary Berry, "Victoria Sponge Sandwich" https://maryberry.co.uk/recipes/recipe/victoria-sponge-sandwich

## CK4. Reverse creaming (paste or two-stage method)
- **Rule (sequence):**
  1. Whisk all the dry ingredients **including the sugar**.
  2. Add **softened butter in about 1 cm cubes** (and some of the liquid in Beranbaum's version) and mix on low **until sandy or pebbly**.
  3. Add the remaining liquids **in stages on medium-low**, scraping between additions.
  4. Add the eggs. **Beat until smooth and fluffy.** Sugar Geek mixes **2 full min** after adding the liquid to build structure.
- **Why:** butter coats the flour, which slows gluten development and reduces trapped air. The result is a **flat top, a velvety ultra-fine crumb** and a sturdy cake that is hard to overmix. It is ideal for stacked or decorated layers, streusel coffee cakes and filled cupcakes.
- **Failure → fix:** a domed cake with tunnels means the method was done wrong (for example, butter too warm, or liquid added all at once).
- **Sources:** King Arthur, "What is reverse creaming" (Beranbaum quoted) https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2022/03/09/what-is-reverse-creaming-and-why-does-it-make-great-cake · ATK, "How to Reverse Cream" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7575-how-to-reverse-cream · Beranbaum, "My Method for Mixing Layer Cakes" https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2005/12/17/my_method_for_mixing_layer_cak · Sugar Geek Show, "Vanilla Cake" https://sugargeekshow.com/vanilla-cake/ · King Arthur, "Cake mixing methods" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2017/04/28/cake-mixing-methods

## CK5. Egg foams: whipping whites
- **Rule:**
  - Use **grease-free** bowls, with not a trace of yolk.
  - Cream of tartar: **⅛ tsp per 30 g white** (1 tsp per 8 whites), added once the whites are foamy.
  - **Add sugar gradually once the whites are foamy** (about 1 min of whipping), **before peaks form**. ATK found this best for meringues, angel food and chiffon.
  - Whip to the stage the recipe needs:
    - **Soft, moist, billowy peaks** for folding into sponge.
    - **Just stiff and glossy** for chiffon (about 1 min more).
  - Never whip until dry.
- **Why:** proteins unfold and cross-link around the air bubbles, and dissolved sugar thickens the water so the foam holds. Sugar added too early weakens the network, so the cake is dry and fine-crumbed. Added too late, it pulls water out of the foam (dense, flat chiffon) or stays grainy.
- **Failure → fix:** if the whites won't whip, there is fat or yolk present, so wipe the bowl with vinegar. If they are lumpy and hard to fold, they are over-whipped. Stiff whites are "very difficult to fold".
- **Sources:** ATK, "Whipping Whites and Sugar: Timing Matters" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/9992-whipping-whites-and-sugar-timing-matter · Beranbaum, "Rose's Tips for Cake Baking" https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2009/11/16/roses_tips_for_cake_baking · ATK, "Foolproof Sponge Cake" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/1012-foolproof-sponge-cake · ATK, "Chiffon Cake" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/6102-chiffon-cake
- **Disagreement:** the ATK chiffon recipe whips the whites to soft peaks *before* adding 2 tbsp sugar, while ATK's own test favours adding sugar at the foamy stage. **Pick:** use the foamy stage for new recipes, unless you are copying a tested recipe exactly.

## CK6. Sponge and chiffon (foam cakes)
- **Sponge (ATK, 2 × 20–23 cm pans, 175 °C):**
  1. Beat the yolks and whole eggs with sugar on medium-high **until very thick and pale, about 5 min** (it forms ribbons).
  2. Combine with the whites whipped to soft peaks.
  3. Sprinkle the flour over and **fold very gently, about 12 strokes**. Add warm milk and butter down one side, then fold **about 8 more strokes** until no flour shows.
  4. Pan and bake **immediately, about 16–20 min**, until light brown and springing back.
  5. **Unmould immediately.**
- **Chiffon (ATK, 16-cup ungreased tube pan, 165 °C):**
  1. Whisk the oil, yolks and water into the dry ingredients.
  2. Lighten with ⅓ of the whites, then fold in the rest.
  3. Bake **55–65 min**, until a toothpick comes out clean and the cracks look dry.
  4. **Cool inverted for about 3 h.**
- **Why:** air in the eggs is the main leavening. Folding keeps the bubbles. Foam cakes in tube pans are cooled upside down because the fragile crumb collapses under its own weight while warm. An ungreased pan gives the batter something to climb.
- **Failure → fix:** a flat, rubbery layer at the base means the foam was deflated or the flour was under-folded into streaks. If the cake collapses after baking, it was underbaked or not inverted. Don't move sponges in the oven until they are fully baked.
- **Sources:** ATK, "Foolproof Sponge Cake" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/1012-foolproof-sponge-cake · ATK, "Chiffon Cake" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/6102-chiffon-cake · King Arthur, "Cake mixing methods" (sponge ribbons, folding) https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2017/04/28/cake-mixing-methods · Beranbaum, "Rose's Tips for Cake Baking" (tube cakes suspended upside down until completely cool; sponge layers unmoulded immediately) https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2009/11/16/roses_tips_for_cake_baking

## CK7. Oven temperature and doming
- **Rule:** most butter layers bake at **165–180 °C (325–350 °F)**. A lower temperature (Sugar Geek uses **168 °C / 335 °F**) or **cake strips** gives a flatter top and moister edges. **Rotate only after about ¾ of the bake time**, never sponges.
- **Why:** the edges set before the centre rises, so the centre domes. Slower, more even heating reduces this.
- **Sources:** Sugar Geek Show, "Vanilla Cake" https://sugargeekshow.com/vanilla-cake/ · Beranbaum, "Rose's Tips for Cake Baking" https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2009/11/16/roses_tips_for_cake_baking · Mary Berry (180 °C / 160 °C fan) https://maryberry.co.uk/recipes/recipe/victoria-sponge-sandwich

## CK8. Doneness cues
- **Rule:** check that **all** of the following are true:
  1. The **edges pull away** from the greased pan by 3–6 mm (this doesn't apply to ungreased foam pans).
  2. It **smells fragrant**.
  3. The top is **golden**, on light cakes.
  4. A **toothpick or paring knife in the centre comes out clean**, or with a few moist crumbs for fudgy cakes, never wet batter.
  5. The centre **springs back** when pressed, leaving no dent.
  - **Thermometer (optional):** most cakes are **93–99 °C (200–210 °F)** in the centre. Treat this as a guide, not a guarantee.
- **Failure → fix:** if the cake is gummy or sinks in the middle, it was underbaked, so extend in 3–5 min steps. If it is dry and the edges are hard, it was overbaked or baked in glass, so check 5 min early.
- **Sources:** King Arthur, "How to tell when cake is done" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2019/07/01/how-to-tell-when-cake-is-done · ATK, "Baking Basics: Testing for Doneness" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/5584-baking-basics-testing-for-doneness · Sugar Geek Show, "Vanilla Cake" (toothpick and spring-back) https://sugargeekshow.com/vanilla-cake/

## CK9. Cooling and unmoulding
- **Rule:**
  - **Butter layers:** cool in the pan on a rack **about 10 min**, then turn out and cool completely before frosting.
  - **Sponge layers:** unmould **immediately**.
  - **Chiffon and angel food:** cool **upside down until completely cool** (about 3 h).
  - **Cheesecake:** see CK10.
  - **Frost only fully cooled layers.** Apply a thin crumb coat first.
- **Sources:** Beranbaum, "Rose's Tips for Cake Baking" https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2009/11/16/roses_tips_for_cake_baking · ATK, "Chiffon Cake" and "Foolproof Sponge Cake" (above) · Sugar Geek Show, "Vanilla Cake" (cool until barely warm before wrapping and chilling) https://sugargeekshow.com/vanilla-cake/

## CK10. Cheesecake: mixing, baking, cracking
- **Rule:**
  - **Mixing:** all dairy and eggs **at room temperature**. Beat the cream cheese smooth first, then add the sugar, then add the eggs **one at a time, just until combined**. Don't whip in air.
  - **Baking method A (water bath):** wrap the springform in foil. **Pour hot water halfway up the pan.** Bake at **160–165 °C (325 °F)**. Dorie's tall cheesecake bakes about 1½ h, then sits **in the turned-off oven with the door propped open for 1 h**.
  - **Baking method B (no bath, Stella Parks):** bake 20 min at 230 °C to puff the cheesecake. Then **turn off the oven and vent it with the door ajar for 10 min**. Finish at **120 °C (250 °F)**. The low temperature replaces the bath.
  - **Doneness:** use a thermometer. **The centre should read 63–68 °C (145–155 °F)**: Stella pulls at 63 °C, 5 cm deep, and allows about 5–6 °C of carryover. King Arthur aims for 66–68 °C at the centre and 74–77 °C 2.5–5 cm from the edge. **It must stay below 85 °C (185 °F) everywhere.** Without a thermometer, the edge should look set and a **5–8 cm centre ring should wobble like gelatin**.
  - **Cooling:** cool slowly. Run a knife around the edge after about 15 min, cool at room temperature 1–4 h, then **refrigerate at least 4 h (Dorie) and preferably 12 h or overnight (Stella)**. Never serve it the same day without a long chill.
- **Why:** egg proteins overheated past about 85 °C tighten into a rigid network that cracks as the cake rises or shrinks. Whipped-in air turns to steam pockets. A sudden temperature change makes the cake contract unevenly.
- **Failure → fix:**
  - Cracks or a browned edge mean it was overbaked, so use a thermometer and lower the heat.
  - Bubbles or cracks from mixing mean it was overmixed, so beat on low.
  - A crack while cooling means a fast temperature change, so cool it in the turned-off oven with the door propped.
  - If it cracks anyway, cover it with fruit or sour cream topping.
- **Disagreement:** water bath (Dorie, King Arthur) vs no bath at a low oven (Stella). **Pick:** either, but the recipe must state one. Use the water bath with a thermometer as the default. Both cite overbaking as the main cause of cracking.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats / Stella Parks, "Epic New York Cheesecake" https://www.seriouseats.com/epic-new-york-cheesecake-from-bravetart · NYT Cooking, "Tall and Creamy Cheesecake" (adapted from Dorie Greenspan) https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1015672-tall-and-creamy-cheesecake · King Arthur, "Why did my cheesecake crack?" https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2024/05/09/why-did-my-cheesecake-crack · Beranbaum (cut with taut dental floss) https://www.realbakingwithrose.com/blog/2009/11/16/roses_tips_for_cake_baking

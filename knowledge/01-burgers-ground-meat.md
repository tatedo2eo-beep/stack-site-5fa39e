# 01 · Burgers and ground meat

> **Private reference. Not for publication.** Chef and outlet names stay in this file only and never go on the site.
> Every source below was opened and checked on 22 Oct 2026 (Africa/Cairo). All sources are on `SOURCES-LOCKED.md`. Tags: **[V]** means the text comes from the written companion of an official chef video. I did not watch the video.
> Units: metric first. "Kosher salt" in US sources is Diamond Crystal unless noted (1 tsp ≈ 3 g; Morton 1 tsp ≈ 4.8 g; table salt 1 tsp ≈ 6 g). Write recipes in grams.

**Site safety override:** our recipes cook all ground beef to **71 °C / 160 °F** in the centre (FSIS, FoodSafety.gov). Several chef sources below cook burgers medium-rare. We keep their crust and handling technique, but never their doneness.

## B1. Fat content of the beef
- **Rule:** use **20 % fat (80/20)** beef as the default for smash, griddle and pan burgers. 15 % fat (85/15) is the leanest acceptable option, for thick grilled, steamed or slider patties. Never go below 15 % fat for burgers. Restaurant smash blends run at about 25 %.
- **Why:** the fat renders onto the pan and fries the crust. It also keeps a thin patty juicy, even though it must cook through. Lean beef turns dry and crumbly at 71 °C.
- **Cue:** you should see a thin film of rendered fat sizzling under the patty within about 30 s. With no fat film, the beef is too lean or the pan is too cold.
- **Failure → fix:** a dry, grey, crumbly patty means the beef is too lean, so switch to 80/20. A greasy, soggy bun means too much fat was carried over, so blot the patty briefly or toast the bun better (B9).
- **Sources:** Serious Eats / J. Kenji López-Alt, "Classic Smashed Burgers" (at least 20 % fat) https://www.seriouseats.com/classic-smashed-burgers-recipe · Serious Eats, "10 Tips for Better Burgers" (at least 20 %) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-labs-top-ten-tips-for-better-burgers · Serious Eats, "Ultimate Smash Cheeseburger" (restaurant blend about 25 %) https://www.seriouseats.com/ultra-smashed-cheeseburger-recipe-food-lab · America's Test Kitchen, "Diner-Style Patty Melts" (80 % lean) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/14928-diner-style-patty-melts · ATK, "Sliders" (85 % lean) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11136-sliders · Gordon Ramsay, "10 Million Subscriber Burger" (85/15 chuck and brisket) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gr10million/

## B2. When to salt
- **Rule:** salt only the **outside** of the formed patty, **just before it goes in the pan** (or salt the top right after smashing). Never knead salt into burger meat, and never salt patties 30 min or more ahead.
- **Why:** salt dissolves the muscle protein myosin. It forms a sticky gel that binds the meat into a springy, sausage-like texture. The longer the contact and the more it is mixed in, the tighter and bouncier the burger gets.
- **Cue:** a tender burger breaks into loose, open-textured pieces. A rubbery, uniform "sausage" cut face means salt went in too early.
- **Failure → fix:** for a bouncy or sausage-like texture, move the salt to the surface at the last minute.
- **Exception:** a **panade** burger (bread and milk mash) for thick, well-done patties mixes in salt with the panade (ATK). The panade keeps it tender at well-done. Use this method only for thick patties, never for smash patties.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats / Kenji, "Salting Ground Beef" https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-lab-salting-ground-beef · ATK, "When to Salt Burgers" (salt 30 min ahead is already springy at the surface) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6586-when-to-salt-burgers · Meathead / AmazingRibs, "Dry Brining" (too much salt in burgers gels the proteins and makes a dense patty) https://amazingribs.com/tested-recipes/salting-brining-curing-and-injecting/dry-brining/ · ATK, "Stovetop Well-Done Hamburgers" (panade exception) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/3307-stovetop-well-done-hamburgers

## B3. How much salt
- **Rule:** use **about 0.8–1.0 % of the raw beef weight** in salt, all on the surface, so about **1 g fine salt per 113 g (4 oz) patty**. Season fattier beef more heavily and leaner meat more lightly.
- **Why:** fat dulls the taste of salt. In ATK's taste test, 80/20 beef needed about 1 % salt by cooked weight to taste seasoned. 90/10 beef and strip steak needed 0.75 %, and lean turkey or pork loin needed 0.5 %.
- **Cross-check:** ATK's slider recipe uses 1½ tsp kosher salt for 680 g beef, about 0.7 % (Diamond) to 1 % (Morton). Kenji's salting experiment used 2 % per patty, but that was a test of texture, not a seasoning recommendation. **Disagreement:** take the lower, tested ATK figure (0.8–1 %). Toppings such as cheese, pickles and sauce add salt too.
- **Failure → fix:** if the burger tastes flat with sauce, the patty is under-salted, so salt the patty, not the sauce. If it tastes salty with American cheese and pickles, drop to 0.8 %.
- **Sources:** ATK, "Does Fattier Meat Need More Salt?" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6597-does-fattier-meat-need-more-salt · ATK, "Sliders" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11136-sliders · Serious Eats, "Salting Ground Beef" https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-lab-salting-ground-beef

## B4. Keep it cold, handle it little
- **Rule:** keep the beef in the fridge until you form it. Form patties gently and only just enough to hold together. Chill the formed patties for at least 10 min, or for up to 24 h, covered. Never add egg, breadcrumbs or onion to a classic patty (the panade burger in B2 is the only exception).
- **Why:** warm fat smears onto your hands and the board and leaves the patty. Working the meat cross-links the proteins and makes the burger dense. Chilled patties hold their shape on the heat.
- **Cue:** a properly formed puck looks loose and slightly ragged, not smooth and compacted.
- **Failure → fix:** for a dense patty, handle less and chill longer. If patties fall apart, chill them firm (Ramsay: chill at least 10 min, or overnight to help them hold their shape).
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "10 Tips for Better Burgers" https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-labs-top-ten-tips-for-better-burgers · Gordon Ramsay, "10 Million Subscriber Burger" (chill at least 10 min) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gr10million/ · ATK, "Sliders" (shape up to 24 h ahead, refrigerated) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11136-sliders · Serious Eats, "The Principles of Topping Burgers" (mix-ins make a meatloaf sandwich) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-lab-the-principles-of-topping-burgers

## B5. Pan and heat for crust (Maillard)
- **Rule:** use heavy **cast iron, carbon steel or stainless steel**, never non-stick at high heat. Preheat for about 5 min over medium, then turn to high until the pan just smokes. Use only a thin film of oil wiped on with paper (about ½ tsp for a 30 cm pan). Restaurant griddles run at 315–370 °C (600–700 °F) for ultra-thin patties.
- **Why:** browning (Maillard) reactions need surface temperatures of about **150 °C / 300 °F and above**, and they speed up as it gets hotter. A thick, well-preheated pan holds its heat when cold meat lands on it. Non-stick coatings break down at these temperatures.
- **Cue:** the pan should give off a wisp of smoke before the meat goes in. The meat should give a loud, steady sizzle the moment it lands. The crust should be deep brown with lacy edges.
- **Failure → fix:** if the patty is grey, steaming and stuck, the pan was not hot enough or was crowded, so cook 2 patties at a time in a 30 cm pan. If the crust burns before it browns evenly on a thick patty, the heat is too high for that thickness, so smash thinner or lower the heat.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "Classic Smashed Burgers" (Maillard at ~150 °C; heavy pan; no non-stick) https://www.seriouseats.com/classic-smashed-burgers-recipe · Serious Eats, "Ultimate Smash Cheeseburger" (315–370 °C) https://www.seriouseats.com/ultra-smashed-cheeseburger-recipe-food-lab · ATK, "Shimmer, Deep-Fry, and Smoke" (heat oil to smoking for quick, thorough browning) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/2977-shimmer-deep-fry-and-smoke-how-oil-behaves-at-different-temperatures

## B6. The smash: timing and thickness
- **Rule:** start from a cold, loose puck (113 g / 4 oz, about 5 cm tall). Put it in the smoking pan and **smash firmly within the first 30 seconds**, **once**. Smash to 10–11 cm across and ≤ 1 cm thick for a classic patty. For a double or ultra-thin patty use 2 × 57 g, smashed as thin as possible. **Never press again** after that first smash. A second spatula pressed on top of the first adds force (Serious Eats).
- **Why:** while the meat is cold, the fat is solid and the juices are locked in the muscle, so pressing loses little. Kenji found that patties smashed within 30 s lost about 20 % of their weight, the same as unsmashed patties. Patties smashed after 1 min lost **about 50 % more moisture**, and repeated smashing can cost half the weight.
- **Cue:** smashed patties have ragged, lacy edges. Leave them alone until the underside is a deep golden brown and the juices bubble on top. That takes **about 1½–2½ min** on a 4 oz patty and about 1 min on a 2 oz ultra-thin one.
- **Failure → fix:** a dry burger means it was smashed late or twice, so smash once, early. A burger that sticks and tears was flipped before the crust formed, so wait for the brown edges.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "Classic Smashed Burgers" (smash within 30 s; ~1½ min first side) https://www.seriouseats.com/classic-smashed-burgers-recipe · Serious Eats, "The Fake Shack" (2 min, 10 cm patties) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-fake-shack-shake-shack-burger-recipe · Serious Eats, "Ultimate Smash Cheeseburger" (2 × 2 oz doubles the crust) https://www.seriouseats.com/ultra-smashed-cheeseburger-recipe-food-lab · Serious Eats, "Wursthall-Style Smashed Burger" [V] (2–2.25 oz patties; double surface area) https://www.seriouseats.com/how-to-make-wursthall-smashburger · ATK, "Sliders" (2 oz patties pressed to 10 cm; 2 min first side) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11136-sliders

## B7. Scrape and flip; second side
- **Rule:** slide a stiff, sharp-edged metal spatula or bench scraper under the patty, pressing down hard so the whole crust comes with it, then flip **once**. On the second side, add cheese at once and cook **30–60 s** for thin patties, or until the burger reaches 71 °C (thicker patties, see B8).
- **Why:** the browned crust is the main point of a smash burger. A weak, flexible spatula leaves it stuck to the pan.
- **Failure → fix:** if the crust stays in the pan, use a stiffer spatula or scraper and push down into the pan as you scrape.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "Classic Smashed Burgers" https://www.seriouseats.com/classic-smashed-burgers-recipe · Serious Eats, "Ultimate Smash Cheeseburger" (razor scraper; second side only seconds) https://www.seriouseats.com/ultra-smashed-cheeseburger-recipe-food-lab · Serious Eats, "Oklahoma-Style Onion Burgers" (pick up all browned meat) https://www.seriouseats.com/oklahoma-onion-burger-recipe

## B8. Doneness and food safety
- **Rule:** ground beef must reach **71 °C / 160 °F** (FSIS and FoodSafety.gov; no rest time needed). For patties ≥ 1.5 cm thick, probe **from the side into the centre**. Thin smash patties (≤ 1 cm) reach 71 °C after a full browning on side one plus 30–60 s on side two. State the 71 °C check in the recipe anyway. Colour is not a reliable sign of doneness.
- **Chef disagreement:** Kenji targets 54 °C (medium-rare), and Ramsay and the Bread Street Kitchen recipe give "2 min a side for medium". **We do not use these.** Our site cooks to 71 °C. That is why we use 80/20 beef and thin patties, which stay juicy when cooked through.
- **Thick patty times (cross-check):** ATK pan-cooks 2 cm (¾ in) panade patties about 5 min, then 4–5 min after flipping for medium-well to well-done. The panade keeps them juicy.
- **Sources:** USDA FSIS, Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart (ground meats 160 °F / 71.1 °C) https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart · FoodSafety.gov chart (ground meat 160 °F / 71 °C) https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures · Serious Eats, "10 Tips" (160 °F = well-done; carryover on patties of 8 oz and up) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-labs-top-ten-tips-for-better-burgers · ATK, "Stovetop Well-Done Hamburgers" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/3307-stovetop-well-done-hamburgers

## B9. Dimple or not
- **Rule:** **dimple** thick patties (press a shallow dip in the centre) **only when grilling or broiling**. Don't bother for skillet or griddle burgers. Form patties about 1 cm wider than the bun, because they shrink.
- **Why:** collagen shrinks above about 60 °C (140 °F). On a grill or under a broiler the edges get hot too, cinch in and push the middle up into a dome. In a skillet only the bottom gets hot enough.
- **Sources:** ATK, "Dimpling Hamburgers" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/5875-dimpling-hamburgers · Gordon Ramsay, "10 Million Subscriber Burger" (form 10 cm × 2.5 cm so it shrinks to bun size) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gr10million/

## B10. Cheese melt
- **Rule:** lay the cheese on **right after the flip**. For American cheese on a thin patty, about 1 min of residual heat is enough. For thicker patties, or cheddar, Swiss and other natural cheeses, **cover**: put a lid on, or add **about 2 tbsp (30 ml) water** to the pan (not on the bun) and lid it for **60–90 s**. Ramsay covers with a bowl and adds a small knob of butter. On grills, close the lid for 1–2 min. For a double, put the cheese **between** the two patties.
- **Why:** American cheese contains **emulsifying salts** (sodium citrate or phosphate) that keep the proteins from tightening, so it melts glossy and never turns greasy. Young, moist cheeses (mild cheddar, Monterey Jack) melt well. Aged or sharp cheddar loses moisture and tightens its proteins, so it "breaks" into grease. Steam or a lid traps heat on top, so the cheese melts before the patty overcooks.
- **Cue:** the cheese should be fully draped over the edges of the patty and glossy, not shiny with separated fat.
- **Failure → fix:** if the cheese is still a stiff square, cover the pan or steam it. If it is greasy and split, the cheese is too aged or too hot for too long, so switch to a young cheese or blend it with American.
- **Sources:** ATK, "Sliders" (2 tbsp water, cover, ~90 s) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11136-sliders · ATK, "Stovetop Well-Done Hamburgers" (cheese ~2 min before done, lid on) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/3307-stovetop-well-done-hamburgers · Gordon Ramsay, "Burger in 10 Minutes" [V] (knob of butter, cover with a bowl) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gordons-burger-in-10-minutes · Gordon Ramsay, "10 Million Subscriber Burger" (close the lid 1–2 min) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gr10million/ · Serious Eats, "The Science of Melting Cheese" https://www.seriouseats.com/the-science-of-melting-cheese · ATK, "Getting to Know: Cooking Cheeses" (young cheddar melts better; old cheddar can curdle) https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6536-getting-to-know-cooking-cheeses · Serious Eats, "Ultimate Smash Cheeseburger" (cheese between two patties) https://www.seriouseats.com/ultra-smashed-cheeseburger-recipe-food-lab

## B11. Bun choice and toasting
- **Rule:** brush the **cut sides only** lightly with melted butter. Toast cut side down on a medium-hot pan, griddle or under the grill **for about 1–2 min, until golden**. **Toast before you cook the patties**: smash patties take under 3 min, and the cooked meat must not wait. Use soft potato or standard buns for thin patties, and a sturdier bun for thick, juicy ones. Avoid tough-crusted or very chewy rolls.
- **Why:** a toasted, browned face resists soaking from juices and sauce and adds toasty flavour.
- **Cue:** the cut face is evenly light-golden with no dark spots.
- **Failure → fix:** if the bun burns at the edges, the heat is too high, so drop to medium. If the bottom bun is soggy, it was untoasted, or the sauce went straight onto bare bread. Toast it, and put lettuce or cheese between the sauce and a very juicy patty.
- **Sequencing:** if you toast in the burger pan, toast first, wipe the pan, then reheat it to smoking for the meat.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "The Fake Shack" (lightly buttered, toast until golden, about 1 min) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-fake-shack-shake-shack-burger-recipe · Gordon Ramsay, "Burger in 10 Minutes" [V] (toast buns 1–2 min) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gordons-burger-in-10-minutes · Gordon Ramsay, "10 Million Subscriber Burger" (lightly butter brioche, low-heat side, watch they don't burn) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gr10million/ · Gordon Ramsay Restaurants, "Bread Street Kitchen Burger" (toast lightly under the grill) https://www.gordonramsayrestaurants.com/recipes/bread-street-kitchen-burger/ · Serious Eats, "10 Tips" (bun choice; toasting reduces soaking) https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-labs-top-ten-tips-for-better-burgers

## B12. Steamed buns and onion-smashed burgers
- **Rule (Oklahoma onion):** smash the raw ball. At once, press a handful of **paper-thin onion** (about ½ medium onion per 70 g patty) hard into the raw top. Cook undisturbed for **about 2 min**, then flip so the onions are underneath. Cook about 1 min, add cheese, then set **both bun halves** on top to steam in the onion vapour until soft.
- **Rule (sliders, steam):** after the flip add cheese and the top buns. Pour **2 tbsp water** into the pan, away from the buns, cover and cook **about 90 s**.
- **Why:** pressed-in onion caramelises against the pan, then steams in the meat's fat. The vapour softens the buns.
- **Failure → fix:** if the onions fall off, they were pressed in too late or cut too thick. Slice them paper-thin and press while the patty is raw.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "Oklahoma-Style Onion Burgers" https://www.seriouseats.com/oklahoma-onion-burger-recipe · ATK, "Sliders" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11136-sliders

## B13. Burger sauces (special sauce family)
- **Rule (base ratio):** a **mayo base (about 4 parts)** + **sweet-tangy condiment (about 1 part: ketchup and/or mustard)** + **an acid or pickle element** (chopped dill pickle, relish or vinegar) + **a dry spice accent** (garlic powder, paprika, cayenne or black pepper). Taste for salt. Chill and keep **up to 5 days** refrigerated.
  - Shack-style: 120 ml mayo, 15 ml ketchup, 15 ml yellow mustard, 4 dill pickle slices chopped fine, ¼ tsp garlic powder, ¼ tsp paprika, a pinch of cayenne; blend until smooth (Serious Eats).
  - Slider sauce: 60 ml mayo, 30 ml ketchup, 1 tsp sweet relish, 1 tsp sugar, 1 tsp white vinegar, 1 tsp black pepper (ATK).
  - Chilli mayo: 60 ml mayo, 30 ml sriracha, a pinch of salt (Ramsay).
- **Why:** the rich mayo carries fat-soluble flavour. The sweet, sour and pickle elements cut through the beef fat (see B14).
- **Failure → fix:** if the sauce is flat, add acid (pickle brine or vinegar) before adding salt. If it is too sweet, cut the ketchup or relish and add mustard.
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "Shack Sauce" https://www.seriouseats.com/shake-shack-sauce-recipe-7107282 · ATK, "Sliders" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/11136-sliders · Gordon Ramsay, "10 Million Subscriber Burger" https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gr10million/

## B14. Building toppings (balance)
- **Rule:** pick toppings from contrasting groups: **sharp or spicy** (raw onion, chilli), **fresh** (lettuce, tomato), **rich** (cheese, bacon, mayo sauces, avocado), **tart or pickled** (pickles, slaw), **sweet** (ketchup, BBQ glaze). A good burger has rich + tart + fresh at least. Toppings go on or under the patty, never mixed into the meat.
- **Order (build; house practice from the sources' builds):** bottom bun → sauce → barrier (lettuce or cheese) → patty → toppings → sauced top bun. Season tomato slices with salt (Ramsay seasons the tomato).
- **Sources:** Serious Eats, "The Principles of Topping Burgers" https://www.seriouseats.com/the-burger-lab-the-principles-of-topping-burgers · Gordon Ramsay, "10 Million Subscriber Burger" (seasoned tomato; sauce on both buns) https://www.gordonramsay.com/gr/recipes/gr10million/

## B15. Patty melt and griddled sandwich style
- **Rule:** use 80/20 beef pressed into **≤ 6 mm** ovals about 6 mm wider than the bread. Cook in a **preheated cast iron pan (5 min, medium-high)** with butter, **60–90 s a side**. Cook onions covered with water until the water is gone, then brown them, about 20 min in all. Assemble with Swiss plus American cheese, then griddle the sandwich in butter over **medium**, covered, **3–5 min + about 3 min**, so the bread is golden and the cheese melted.
- **Sources:** ATK, "Diner-Style Patty Melts" https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/14928-diner-style-patty-melts · Serious Eats, "Classic Smashed Burgers" (thin, hot, early-smash principle) https://www.seriouseats.com/classic-smashed-burgers-recipe

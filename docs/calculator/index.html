Where the numbers come from

  • Calories at rest (BMR): Mifflin-St Jeor equation, men 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5; women the same − 161. Mifflin et al. 1990
  • Activity: BMR × 1.4 / 1.6 / 1.8 / 2.0 (EFSA physical activity levels). EFSA 2017
  • Losing: 500 kcal below maintenance, not below 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men). AHA/ACC/TOS 2013. Gaining: 10% above maintenance. Iraki et al. 2019
  • Protein: lifting 1.6–2.2 g/kg (Morton et al. 2018); exercising 1.4–2.0 g/kg (ISSN 2017); losing weight 1.2–1.6 g/kg (Leidy et al. 2015); everyone else 1.2–1.6 g/kg (US Dietary Guidelines 2025–2030); minimum 0.83 g/kg (EFSA 2012; US RDA 0.8 g/kg); over 65 at least 1.0–1.2 g/kg (PROT-AGE 2013); never more than 35% of calories (US DGA 2020–2025, App. 1).
  • Fat, free sugar, salt, fibre: fat about 30% of calories, saturated fat under 10%, free sugar under 10% (ideally 5%), salt under 5 g, fibre at least 25 g. WHO Healthy diet. Fat never under 20%, carbs 45–60%: EFSA. Fibre 14 g per 1,000 kcal, carbs at least 130 g: US DGA 2020–2025.
  • Grams from calories: 4 kcal per g protein and carbs, 9 kcal per g fat. FAO 2003
  • Water: 2.0 litres (women) / 2.5 litres (men) a day from drinks and food. EFSA

Choosing one number inside each range (for example 1.6 g/kg rather than 2.2 g/kg) and rounding calories to 10 kcal are our own choices.

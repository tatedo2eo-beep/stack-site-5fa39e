Nashville Hot Chicken

Inspired by techniques from Serious Eats and America's Test Kitchen.

Nashville hot chicken · Classic · American (Nashville)

Buttermilk-brined chicken is coated, deep-fried until crisp, then brushed with a blazing cayenne and brown sugar oil and served on white bread with pickles.

Prep25 min
Cook35 min
Total1 h
DifficultyMedium

Ingredients

Metric · 4 servings

Brine

  • 800 g boneless, skinless chicken thighs
  • 300 ml buttermilk
  • 2 tbsp vinegar-based hot sauce
  • 6 g fine sea salt or table salt about 1 tsp

Dredge

  • 150 g plain flour
  • 30 g cornflour
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 3 g fine sea salt or table salt about ½ tsp
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper

For frying

  • 1.2 l neutral frying oil (sunflower, rapeseed or vegetable) for 4-5 cm depth

Hot oil

  • 120 ml hot frying oil, ladled from the pot
  • 20 g unsalted butter
  • 2 tbsp cayenne pepper (1 tbsp for milder)
  • 18 g brown sugar about 1.5 tbsp
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • 0.5 g fine sea salt or table salt about 1 pinch

To serve

  • 4 slices soft white bread
  • 60 g dill pickle slices

Method

  1. 1

    Brine the chicken. Mix the buttermilk, hot sauce and salt, then add the chicken and refrigerate for 1-12 hours. The salty brine seasons the meat right through and helps it stay juicy in the hot oil, and the buttermilk left on the surface helps the dredge stick.

  2. 2

    Mix the dredge. Whisk together the flour, cornflour, baking powder, cayenne, paprika, salt and pepper. Drizzle in 2 tablespoons of the brine and rub it through the flour with your fingers to form small clumps, which is what gives the finished crust its craggy texture.

  3. 3

    Coat and rest. Lift the chicken from the brine, let it drip briefly, then press firmly into the dredge to coat. Rest the coated pieces on the rack for 15 minutes before frying, so the flour has time to hydrate and grip the chicken rather than falling off in the oil.

  4. 4

    Fry the chicken. Heat 4-5 cm of oil to 175°C in the heavy pot. Fry 3 pieces at a time for 6-8 minutes, turning occasionally and keeping the oil between 150-165°C, until deep golden and the chicken reaches 80°C inside, then drain on the rack.

  5. 5

    Make the hot oil. Put the cayenne, sugar, paprika, garlic powder, salt and butter into a heatproof bowl, then carefully ladle in 120 ml of the still-hot frying oil and whisk together. Pouring the hot oil straight over the spices blooms the cayenne, waking up its flavour far more than stirring it in cold would.

  6. 6

    Glaze and serve. While both are still hot, brush or spoon the spiced oil over the chicken so it clings evenly to the crust. Serve each piece on a slice of white bread with pickles underneath - the bread soaks up the fiery oil and the pickles cool the heat down.

Tips

  • Rest the coated chicken for the full 15 minutes before frying so the crust holds together, rather than falling off in clumps in the oil.
  • Keep the oil between 165-175°C throughout frying; letting it drop lower is the easiest way to end up with greasy, soft chicken.
  • Pour the hot oil over the spice mix rather than warming them together in a pan - the sudden heat is what blooms the cayenne's flavour.
  • Brush the chicken with the hot oil immediately after frying, while both are still hot, so the glaze sets onto the crust properly.
  • Reduce the cayenne in the hot oil to 1 tablespoon if you want a milder version - it's easy to misjudge how much heat the full amount brings.
  • Frying safety: use a heavy, deep pot no more than half full of oil, with a clip-on thermometer, and dry the food before it goes in. Never leave hot oil unattended. If the oil catches fire, turn off the heat and cover the pot with a metal lid or use a fire extinguisher; never use water.

Storage

Best eaten fresh, as the hot oil glaze softens the crust over time. If needed, keep for up to 2 days in the fridge and reheat in a hot oven until piping hot (74°C), understanding the crust won't be quite as crisp as when first made. Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours (within 1 hour on a day above 32°C).

Equipment

  • heavy pot (at least 4 litres)
  • frying thermometer
  • wire rack over a tray
  • heatproof bowl
  • ladle
  • pastry brush

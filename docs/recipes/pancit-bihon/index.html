Pancit Bihon (Filipino Rice Vermicelli with Chicken and Vegetables)

Inspired by techniques from Serious Eats and NYT Cooking.

Pancit bihon · Classic · Filipino

Fine rice vermicelli stir-fried with chicken, prawns and crunchy vegetables in a light soy and oyster sauce broth, a classic Filipino noodle dish for sharing.

Prep25 min
Cook20 min
Total45 min
DifficultyEasy

Ingredients

Metric · 4 servings

Noodles

  • 225 g rice vermicelli (bihon)

Protein

  • 400 g skinless chicken thigh fillets, thinly sliced
  • 150 g raw peeled prawns

Stir-fry

  • 2 tbsp neutral oil (rapeseed, sunflower or vegetable)
  • 5 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • ½ red onion, finely chopped
  • 2 carrots, coarsely grated
  • 115 g mangetout, strings removed
  • 150 g white cabbage, shredded

Sauce

  • 250 ml low-salt chicken stock
  • 250 ml water
  • 2 tbsp oyster sauce
  • 1½ tbsp light soy sauce

To finish

  • 2 spring onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 calamansi or 1 lime, cut into wedges

Method

  1. 1

    Soak the vermicelli. Soak the rice vermicelli in warm water for 8-10 minutes, just until the strands turn pliable but not fully soft, then drain and snip into shorter lengths with scissors. Stopping the soak early matters, since over-softened bihon turns mushy as soon as it hits the hot wok.

  2. 2

    Cook chicken and prawns. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in the wok over medium-high heat and stir-fry the chicken for 4-5 minutes until no pink remains and it reaches 74°C at the thickest point, then add the prawns and cook for a further 1-2 minutes until opaque. Lift everything out onto a plate so it doesn't overcook while you move on to the vegetables.

  3. 3

    Soften garlic and onion. Add the remaining oil, garlic and onion to the wok and stir-fry for 1-2 minutes until lightly golden. Cooking these first builds a fragrant base that the vegetables and noodles will cook into.

  4. 4

    Stir-fry the vegetables. Add the carrots, mangetout and cabbage and stir-fry for 2 minutes until bright and just tender, then scoop them out and set aside with the chicken and prawns. Keeping everything crisp at this stage means it won't turn soft and grey once it goes back in with the noodles.

  5. 5

    Simmer the noodles. Pour the stock, water, oyster sauce and soy sauce into the wok and bring to the boil, then add the drained noodles and toss for 3-4 minutes until they turn translucent and have absorbed the liquid. Letting the vermicelli cook directly in the seasoned liquid is what gives pancit bihon its savoury depth right through the noodle rather than just on the surface.

  6. 6

    Combine and finish. Return the chicken, prawns and vegetables to the wok and toss for 1 minute until everything is heated through and evenly mixed. Scatter over the spring onions and serve with calamansi or lime wedges on the side.

Tips

  • Don't oversoak the vermicelli — if it's already fully soft before it reaches the wok, it will fall apart into a sticky mass once tossed with the sauce.
  • Cook the chicken and prawns first and set them aside; adding raw protein at the same time as the delicate noodles risks overcooking one or the other.
  • Toss the noodles constantly once the liquid is added so they absorb it evenly instead of clumping at the bottom of the wok.
  • If the vermicelli still looks dry and separate after 4 minutes, add a splash more water rather than forcing it with more tossing.

Storage

Keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat gently in a pan with a splash of water, or in the microwave, until piping hot (74°C) throughout; freezing isn't recommended as the fine noodles turn soft and clumpy once thawed. Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours (within 1 hour on a day above 32°C).

Equipment

  • large heatproof bowl
  • wok (35 cm) or large non-stick frying pan (30 cm)
  • wok spatula or tongs
  • small bowls for the prepared ingredients

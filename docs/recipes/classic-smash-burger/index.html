Classic double smash burger

Two thin beef patties pressed hard onto a smoking-hot pan so the edges turn lacy and dark, stacked with melting cheddar in a buttery toasted brioche bun. Loud, quick and very much a treat.

Ingredients

2 servings

Patties

  • 300 gbeef mince, 20% fat kept in the fridge until you cook
  • 1 gfine salt
  • to tasteblack pepper, freshly ground
  • 4 slicescheddar cheese about 20 g each

To build

  • 2brioche buns about 70 g each
  • 1 tbspbutter, softened
  • 4little gem lettuce leaves
  • 1ripe tomato about 120 g, sliced

Burger sauce

  • 1 tbspmayonnaise
  • 1 tbsptomato ketchup
  • 15 ggherkin, finely chopped

Equipment

  • Cast-iron pan or flat griddle
  • Stiff flat metal spatula or burger press
  • Baking paper
  • Small bowl

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Cereals containing gluten
  • Eggs
  • Milk
  • Mustard

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Get everything ready

    Slice the tomato, rinse and dry the lettuce, and stir the mayonnaise, ketchup and chopped gherkin together. The cooking takes minutes, so have every part of the burger within reach before the pan goes on.

  2. Portion the beef

    Divide the mince into four loose balls of about 75 g each. Handle them as little as possible and do not pack them tight; put them back in the fridge while you toast the buns.

  3. Toast the buns

    Spread the butter on the cut sides of the buns and toast them, cut side down, in the dry pan over medium heat for about a minute until golden. Set aside.

  4. Smash the patties

    Turn the heat to high and wait until the pan is smoking. Add two balls, lay a square of baking paper on each and press straight down firmly with the spatula for about 10 seconds until each is roughly 1 cm thin with ragged edges. Peel off the paper and season with the salt and pepper.

  5. Flip and melt the cheese

    Leave the patties alone for about 2 minutes until the edges are deep brown and crisp. Scrape firmly under the crust, flip, add a slice of cheese to each and cook for 1 more minute. Stack one patty on another. Repeat with the other two balls.

  6. Build and serve

    Spread sauce on the bottom bun, add lettuce and tomato, then the cheesy double patty and the top bun. Serve straight away while the edges are still crunchy. Beef mince should be cooked through with no pink in the middle (at least 71 °C).

Tips

  • The pan has to be properly hot before the beef goes in. A lukewarm pan steams the meat instead of browning it.
  • Baking paper between the spatula and the meat stops it sticking, so you can press hard without tearing the patty.
  • Season only after smashing; salt mixed into the mince early makes the patties dense and springy.

Storage

Best eaten immediately. Cooked patties keep in a covered container in the fridge for up to 2 days; reheat in a hot pan until piping hot all the way through. Do not store assembled burgers.

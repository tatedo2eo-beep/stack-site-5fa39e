Lemon Garlic Prawn Linguine

Silky lemon and garlic linguine tossed with juicy pan-seared prawns and softened cherry tomatoes, finished with a glossy butter sauce that clings to every strand.

Ingredients

4 servings

Pasta

  • 350 gdried linguine or spaghetti
  • 1 tbspfine salt, for the pasta water in 3 litres of water

Prawns and sauce

  • 400 graw king prawns (shrimp), peeled and deveined thawed and patted dry if frozen
  • ¼ tspfine salt
  • 3 tbspolive oil
  • 6garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • ½ tspchilli flakes
  • 320 gcherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1unwaxed lemon, zest finely grated
  • 3 tbsplemon juice from 1-2 lemons
  • 25 gunsalted butter, cold, cubed
  • 20 gflat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • ½ tspground black pepper

Equipment

  • large pot (at least 4 litres)
  • large, deep frying pan or sauté pan (30 cm)
  • tongs
  • heatproof mug for pasta water
  • fine grater (for zest)

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Cereals containing gluten
  • Crustaceans
  • Milk

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Boil water, dry prawns

    Bring the 3 litres of water to a rolling boil in a large pot with the 1 tablespoon of salt. While it heats, pat the prawns very dry with paper towel and season with the 1/4 teaspoon salt and the pepper — dry prawns sear rather than steam, so they colour properly in the pan.

  2. Cook the pasta

    Add the linguine to the boiling water and stir through during the first minute so the strands don't stick together. Cook for 1-2 minutes less than the packet time, since it finishes cooking in the sauce, and before draining, scoop out a mugful (about 250 ml) of the starchy pasta water — this is what lets the sauce cling to the pasta later.

  3. Sear the prawns

    While the pasta cooks, heat 1 tablespoon of the oil in the 30 cm pan over high heat until shimmering, then add the prawns in a single layer. Cook for 1 minute per side until pink and just opaque, curling into a loose C shape — a tight, curled-up O is a sign they've gone past done and will turn rubbery. Lift them onto a plate; they'll finish cooking later in the sauce.

  4. Soften the garlic

    Lower the heat to medium-low and add the remaining 2 tablespoons of oil along with the sliced garlic, cooking for 1-2 minutes, stirring often, until pale gold and fragrant. Garlic turns bitter the moment it browns, so watch the colour closely; add the chilli flakes for the final 10 seconds just to wake up their flavour in the warm oil. Tip in the cherry tomatoes, raise the heat to medium and cook for 2-3 minutes until they soften and start to release their juices, which add sweetness and body to the sauce.

  5. Build the sauce

    Pour in the lemon juice and 120 ml of the reserved pasta water, bring to a simmer, then add the drained pasta. Toss with tongs over medium heat for 1-2 minutes, adding the cold butter a few cubes at a time — the starch, butter and oil emulsify into a silky sauce that coats the strands rather than pooling at the bottom. Loosen with a splash more pasta water if it looks tight or dry.

  6. Return prawns, serve

    Tip the prawns and their juices back into the pan with the lemon zest and parsley, tossing for 30 seconds just to heat the prawns through so they're opaque all the way through. Taste, adjust the seasoning, and serve immediately while the sauce is still glossy.

Tips

  • Overcooking the prawns is the most common slip here — pull them off the heat the moment they curl into a loose C and let them finish gently in the sauce instead of the pan.
  • Frozen prawns need thawing first: leave them in the fridge overnight, or in a sealed bag under cold water for 20-30 minutes, then pat thoroughly dry before cooking.
  • Slice the garlic thin and even so every piece turns golden at the same rate rather than some burning before others cook through.
  • Extra lemon wedges on the side let everyone add more brightness at the table.
  • There is no cheese here on purpose: grated hard cheese tends to mask the sweet prawn and lemon flavours.

Storage

This dish is best eaten fresh, but leftovers will keep in the fridge for up to 1 day. Reheat gently in a pan with a splash of water until piping hot, and reheat only once, as prawns toughen quickly on a second heating. Not suitable for freezing.

Fluffy Greek Yoghurt Pancakes

Thick, fluffy pancakes made with Greek yoghurt for a gentle tang and a good protein boost, with no added sugar, served warm with more yoghurt and fresh berries.

Ingredients

3 servings

Batter

  • 120 gplain flour
  • 1 tspbaking powder
  • ½ tspbicarbonate of soda (baking soda)
  • 1 pinchfine salt
  • ¼ tspground cinnamon
  • 250 gfat-free Greek yoghurt
  • 2large eggs
  • 30 mlmilk (semi-skimmed) plus 1-2 tbsp more if needed
  • 10 gunsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled
  • 1unwaxed lemon, zest finely grated optional
  • 1 tspneutral oil, for the pan

To serve

  • 150 gfat-free Greek yoghurt, to serve
  • 250 gmixed berries, fresh or thawed

Equipment

  • large non-stick frying pan or flat griddle (26-28 cm)
  • 2 mixing bowls and a whisk
  • silicone spatula
  • thin flexible turner
  • ladle or 60 ml measuring scoop

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Cereals containing gluten
  • Eggs
  • Milk

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Whisk the dry ingredients

    Whisk the flour, baking powder, bicarbonate of soda, salt and cinnamon together in a bowl until no streaks remain. Spreading the raising agents evenly matters here, since a stray pocket of bicarbonate of soda can taste faintly soapy in the finished pancake.

  2. Whisk the wet ingredients

    In a separate bowl, whisk the yoghurt, eggs, milk, melted butter and lemon zest (if using) until completely smooth. Look for an even, creamy consistency with no streaks of egg white. This base is what gives the pancakes their rich taste without needing added sugar.

  3. Fold wet into dry

    Tip the dry mixture into the wet and fold gently with a spatula until just combined, leaving a few small lumps rather than a silky batter. As soon as the yoghurt's acidity meets the bicarbonate of soda you'll see tiny bubbles forming, a sign the rise has already started. Overmixing develops the flour's gluten and leaves pancakes tough and flat, so stop as soon as the flour disappears. The batter should be thick enough to drop slowly off a spoon; stir in 1-2 tablespoons more milk if it's too stiff, then rest it for 5 minutes - no longer, since the bicarbonate is already working.

  4. Heat and test the pan

    Warm a non-stick pan over medium heat for 2 minutes, then wipe it with a little oil on a piece of kitchen paper. Drop in a teaspoon of batter as a test: it should sizzle gently and turn golden underneath in about a minute. If the pan is too hot the outside will scorch before the centre cooks through, because the natural sugars in yoghurt brown quickly.

  5. Cook in batches

    Spoon 60 ml scoops of batter into the pan, 3-4 at a time, spreading each slightly to about 8 cm. Cook for 2-2.5 minutes until bubbles appear on the surface, the edges look dry and set, and the underside is golden, then flip once and cook for a further 1-1.5 minutes until springy in the centre. Lower the heat a little if they're browning too fast, and wipe the pan with fresh oil before each new batch.

  6. Keep warm and serve

    Transfer cooked pancakes to a tray in a single layer and hold them in a low oven at 120°C (100°C fan) while you finish the rest. Keeping them in one layer, uncovered, stops trapped steam turning the tops soft. Serve warm topped with the extra yoghurt and mixed berries.

Tips

  • Cook over medium heat rather than high — yoghurt's natural sugars brown quickly, so high heat burns the outside before the middle sets.
  • Resist pressing the pancakes with the spatula while they cook; it pushes out the air bubbles that make them fluffy.
  • Greek yoghurt brands vary in thickness — if your batter won't drop slowly off a spoon, loosen it with an extra tablespoon or two of milk.
  • If using frozen berries, thaw and drain them first so the juice doesn't bleed into the yoghurt.
  • Treat your first pancake as a tester for heat and timing before cooking the rest of the batch.

Storage

Once cooled, pancakes keep in the fridge for 2 days, or freeze for up to 2 months layered between baking paper. Reheat from chilled or frozen in a toaster or dry pan until hot through; add the yoghurt and berries fresh just before serving.

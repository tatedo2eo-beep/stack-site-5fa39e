Chewy Tapioca Cheese Rolls

Inspired by techniques from America's Test Kitchen, Serious Eats and King Arthur Baking.

Pão de queijo · Classic · Brazilian

Soft, cheesy Brazilian-style rolls with a chewy tapioca centre and a crisp golden crust, made from a hot starch paste and baked until well puffed.

Prep20 min
Cook30 min
Total50 min
DifficultyMedium

Ingredients

Metric · 8 servings

Dough

  • 340 g tapioca starch (tapioca flour)
  • 6 g fine sea salt or table salt about 1 tsp
  • ¼ tsp baking powder
  • 200 ml whole milk
  • 120 ml neutral oil
  • 20 g unsalted butter
  • 2 large eggs
  • 100 g Parmesan-style hard cheese, finely grated (check the rennet)
  • 100 g low-moisture mozzarella, grated (check the rennet)

Glaze

  • 1 large egg, beaten with 1 tsp water, to glaze

Method

  1. 1

    Make the hot starch paste. Tip the tapioca starch, salt and baking powder into the mixer bowl. Bring the milk, oil and butter to a full boil in the saucepan, then pour it over the starch with the mixer running on low and beat for 3 minutes until you have a lumpy, glossy paste. The boiling liquid instantly gelatinises the tapioca starch, and that reaction is exactly what gives these rolls their stretchy, chewy bite.

  2. 2

    Cool to warm. Leave the paste for 5 minutes until it feels warm rather than hot to the touch. This pause matters because eggs stirred into a hot mixture will start to cook and scramble, losing their power to bind and lighten the dough.

  3. 3

    Beat in the eggs. Add the eggs one at a time, mixing on low for 6-8 minutes and scraping down the bowl once or twice, or beating hard by hand for 8-10 minutes. Keep going until the dough turns smooth, shiny and sticky, and clings to the side of the bowl rather than sitting in a puddle. Thorough beating develops the egg proteins fully, which traps steam and is what makes the rolls puff up in the oven.

  4. 4

    Fold in the cheeses. Mix in the Parmesan-style cheese and mozzarella until they're evenly spread through the dough. Both cheeses melt as the rolls bake, adding savoury flavour and helping the crust turn a deep golden colour.

  5. 5

    Chill the dough. Press cling film directly onto the surface of the dough and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, or overnight. Chilling firms up the sticky dough so it's far easier to roll, and gives the tapioca starch more time to fully hydrate.

  6. 6

    Shape the rolls. Heat the oven to 230°C (210°C fan). With lightly wet hands, roll the dough into 16 smooth balls of about 55g each and space them out on the lined tray. Damp hands stop the sticky dough sticking to your fingers, so you get neat, even balls.

  7. 7

    Glaze and bake. Brush each ball with the beaten egg glaze, then put the trays in the oven and immediately turn the temperature down to 190°C (170°C fan). Bake for 25-30 minutes, turning the tray halfway, until the rolls are well puffed, deep golden and crisp on the outside. The initial blast of heat kick-starts a strong rise, and the lower temperature that follows bakes them through without scorching the crust.

  8. 8

    Cool and serve. Let the rolls cool for 10 minutes on the wire rack before serving them warm. This short rest allows the soft, cheesy centre to set slightly so the rolls hold their shape when you bite in.

Tips

  • Don't add the eggs while the paste is still hot - wait until it's just warm, or they'll scramble and leave the dough looking grainy instead of smooth and glossy.
  • Weigh the tapioca starch rather than measuring by volume, since even small differences change the dough's stretch and chew.
  • Grate your own cheese instead of using pre-shredded bags, which often contain anti-caking starches that stop it melting smoothly into the dough.
  • If you need the rolls to be gluten-free, double check the cheese and baking powder labels, as some brands use anti-caking agents that contain gluten.
  • Avoid opening the oven door in the first 15 minutes of baking, as a sudden drop in heat can stop the rolls puffing properly.

Storage

Keep cooled rolls in an airtight container for up to 2 days and refresh them in a 180°C oven for 5 minutes. Unbaked balls freeze well: freeze them on a tray until solid, bag them up, then bake straight from frozen, adding 5-10 minutes to the baking time. Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours (within 1 hour on a day above 32°C).

Equipment

  • stand mixer with paddle, or large bowl and sturdy wooden spoon
  • small saucepan
  • cling film
  • 2 stacked baking trays, the top one lined with baking paper
  • pastry brush
  • wire rack

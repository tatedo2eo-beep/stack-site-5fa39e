Big-Batch Beef and Bean Chili

A deeply savoury, slow-simmered beef and bean chili with a rich spiced sauce built from properly browned mince and toasted spices. It's a big-batch dinner perfect for feeding a crowd or freezing in portions.

Ingredients

8 servings
  • 1 kglean beef mince, 5% fat
  • 1 tbspneutral oil
  • 2large onions, finely chopped
  • 1red pepper, diced
  • 4garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1½ tbspmild chili powder blend the US-style seasoning mix, not pure hot chilli powder
  • 2 tspground cumin
  • 2 tspsmoked paprika
  • 1 tspdried oregano
  • ¼ tspground cinnamon
  • 1 tbspunsweetened cocoa powder
  • 2 tbsptomato purée (paste)
  • 800 gchopped tomatoes (2 tins)
  • 480 gdrained tinned red kidney beans (2 tins), rinsed
  • 300 mlwater
  • ¾ tspfine salt then taste at the end
  • ½ tspground black pepper

Equipment

  • large heavy casserole or stockpot (5-6 litres) with lid
  • wooden spoon
  • sieve
  • instant-read thermometer (optional)

Allergens (EU 14)

None of the 14 major EU allergens.

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Brown the beef in batches

    Heat the oil in a 5-6 litre casserole over high heat, then add a third of the mince and press it into a flat layer. Leave it for 3-4 minutes until deeply browned underneath before breaking it up, turning, and cooking for a further 2 minutes; lift out with a slotted spoon and repeat with the remaining two batches. Lean mince has little fat to render, so it needs that oil and plenty of space — crowd the pan and the meat steams instead of browning, losing the rich flavour that proper browning (the Maillard reaction) gives.

  2. Soften onion and pepper

    With the beef out of the pan, add the chopped onions and red pepper to the drippings left behind and cook over medium heat for 8-10 minutes until soft, scraping up any browned bits stuck to the base as you go. Those stuck-on bits are packed with flavour from the beef, and working them back into the vegetables builds the base of the chili.

  3. Toast the spices

    Once the vegetables have softened, stir in the garlic, chili powder blend, cumin, smoked paprika, oregano, cinnamon and cocoa powder, and cook for 1 minute until fragrant, then add the tomato purée and cook for a further minute. Toasting dried spices briefly in the hot fat wakes up their oils and deepens their flavour, while the cocoa adds a savoury background note rather than any sweetness.

  4. Simmer the base

    Return the browned beef to the pan along with the chopped tomatoes, water, salt and pepper. Bring to the boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer with the lid slightly ajar and cook for 45 minutes, stirring every 10-15 minutes and scraping the bottom so the thickening sauce doesn't catch and burn.

  5. Stir in the beans

    Fifteen minutes before serving, add the drained kidney beans and simmer uncovered until thick and rich — a spoon drawn through the chili should leave a path that slowly fills back in. Loosen with a splash of water if it looks too thick; by now the beef is well beyond the 71°C needed for safety. Adding the beans this late keeps them intact rather than breaking down into mush.

  6. Rest and serve

    Taste and adjust the salt, then let the chili rest off the heat for 10 minutes so the flavours can settle and it cools enough not to scald straight from the pot. Serve with rice, tortilla chips or baked potatoes and your choice of toppings.

Tips

  • UK chilli powder is hot, not the mild US-style blend this recipe needs — if that's all you have, swap in 1 tsp hot chilli powder plus an extra 2 tsp smoked paprika and 1 extra tsp cumin.
  • Don't skip browning the beef in batches: crowding the pan makes it steam and turn grey instead of developing deep flavour.
  • This chili tastes even better the next day once the spices have had time to mellow and meld.
  • Adjust the heat at the end with a pinch of chilli flakes rather than loading up the spice mix earlier.
  • Stir the beans in only for the last 15 minutes so they hold their shape instead of turning mushy.

Storage

Cool within 2 hours, then keep in the fridge for up to 4 days or freeze in portions for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat until piping hot throughout (74°C), stirring in a splash of water if needed.

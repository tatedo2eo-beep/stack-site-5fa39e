Grilled Beef Kofta with Tahini Yoghurt

Spiced beef kofta are kneaded until sticky, moulded onto skewers and grilled until charred, then served with a creamy tahini yoghurt sauce.

Ingredients

4 servings

Kofta

  • 500 gbeef mince, 15% fat or half beef, half lamb mince
  • 1medium onion, coarsely grated about 100 g
  • 20 gflat-leaf parsley, very finely chopped
  • 2garlic cloves, finely grated
  • 1½ tspground cumin
  • 1 tspground coriander
  • 1 tspsweet paprika
  • ½ tspground allspice
  • ¼ tspground cinnamon
  • ¾ tspfine salt
  • ½ tspground black pepper
  • 1 tspneutral oil, for brushing

Tahini yoghurt

  • 150 gfat-free Greek yoghurt
  • 30 gtahini, well stirred
  • 1 tbsplemon juice
  • 1small garlic clove, finely grated
  • 1 pinchfine salt
  • 1 tbspcold water, to loosen

Equipment

  • box grater
  • clean tea towel or sieve
  • large bowl
  • 8 metal skewers (or wooden ones soaked for 30 min)
  • ridged griddle pan, barbecue or grill (broiler) with a rack
  • instant-read thermometer

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Milk
  • Sesame

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Drain the onion

    The onion needs grating, then squeezing hard in a clean tea towel or pressing through a sieve to remove as much juice as possible. A wet onion makes the kofta mixture soft and slide straight off the skewers, so draining it keeps the sweetness without the excess liquid.

  2. Mix and knead

    Combine the beef, drained onion, parsley, garlic, spices, salt and pepper in a bowl. Knead firmly with your hand for 2 minutes, until the mixture turns sticky and slightly paler in colour. The salt dissolves proteins in the muscle that act like a glue, which is what lets the kofta hold together on the skewer without needing egg or breadcrumbs.

  3. Chill the mixture

    Covered and refrigerated for at least 30 minutes, and up to 24 hours, the mixture firms up its fat, which makes it noticeably easier to shape. This short rest also lets the spices settle through the meat.

  4. Shape onto skewers

    Divide the mixture into 8 portions of about 75 g each. With damp hands, mould each portion around a skewer into a sausage shape 12-14 cm long and about 2.5 cm thick, pressing firmly so it grips, and leave a slight dimple pattern with your fingertips. Keeping the thickness even along the length means the kofta cook through at the same rate.

  5. Make the sauce

    Whisk the tahini with the lemon juice, garlic and salt in a small bowl. It will seize into a stiff paste at first — completely normal, as the acid tightens the sesame proteins. Keep whisking in the water a little at a time until it loosens into a smooth, creamy sauce, then fold it through the yoghurt.

  6. Grill the kofta

    Heat a ridged griddle pan over medium-high for 5 minutes, or heat a barbecue or grill to high. Brush the kofta lightly with oil and cook for 10-12 minutes, turning every 2-3 minutes once each side releases easily from the pan, until well browned all over and the centre reads 71°C with no pink remaining and clear juices. Turning only once each side has released keeps the crust intact rather than tearing it off too early.

  7. Rest and serve

    Rest the kofta for 2-3 minutes before serving with the tahini yoghurt, warm flatbreads, sliced tomato, onion and fresh herbs.

Tips

  • Don't try to turn the kofta too soon — the meat releases cleanly from the pan on its own once a crust has formed underneath.
  • If the mixture feels too soft to hold its shape on the skewer, chill it for longer rather than adding breadcrumbs.
  • Soak wooden skewers in water for 30 minutes before using them, so they don't scorch and burn under the grill.
  • Minced meat needs to reach a higher temperature than a whole steak — cook kofta to 71°C rather than treating them like rare steak.
  • Mince with around 15-20% fat stays noticeably juicier on the grill than very lean mince, which can turn dry and crumbly.

Storage

Raw shaped kofta can be covered and kept in the fridge for up to 24 hours, or frozen for up to 3 months and thawed in the fridge overnight. Cooked kofta keep in the fridge for 3 days; reheat until piping hot (74°C). The tahini yoghurt keeps for 3 days in the fridge.

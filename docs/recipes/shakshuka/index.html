Smoky Shakshuka with Feta

Peppers and onion melt into a smoky paprika-spiced tomato sauce, topped with eggs poached right in the pan and finished with crumbled feta.

Ingredients

3 servings
  • 2 tbspolive oil
  • 1medium onion, thinly sliced
  • 2red peppers, deseeded and thinly sliced about 300 g
  • 3garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 1 tspground cumin
  • 2 tspsmoked or sweet paprika
  • ½ tspchilli flakes or to taste
  • 1 tbsptomato purée (paste)
  • 800 gchopped tomatoes (2 tins)
  • ½ tspfine salt feta adds more saltiness
  • ½ tspground black pepper
  • 6large eggs
  • 60 gfeta, crumbled
  • 10 gfresh coriander or parsley, chopped

Equipment

  • deep frying pan or sauté pan with a lid (28 cm)
  • wooden spoon
  • ramekin or small bowl

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Eggs
  • Milk

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Soften Onion and Pepper

    Heat the oil in the 28 cm deep pan over medium heat, then add the onion and peppers with a pinch of the salt. Cook for 10-12 minutes, stirring often, until very soft and starting to turn sweet and faintly golden at the edges — that slow cooking is what turns the pepper's raw sharpness into sweetness.

  2. Bloom the Spices

    Stir in the garlic, cumin, paprika and chilli flakes and cook for 1 minute. The gentle heat releases the spices' fat-soluble aromas straight into the oil, but watch the paprika closely, as it turns bitter if it catches and burns.

  3. Simmer the Sauce

    Stir in the tomato purée and cook for 1 minute, then add the chopped tomatoes with the remaining salt and the pepper. Simmer uncovered over medium-low heat for 12-15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thick enough that a spoon dragged through leaves a clear trail for a second or two — too thin a sauce and the eggs will sink and spread into watery whites.

  4. Add the Eggs

    Use the back of a spoon to make 6 hollows in the sauce. Crack each egg into a ramekin or small bowl first to check for shell or a broken yolk, then slide it gently into a well, seasoning the eggs with a little pepper.

  5. Cook Covered

    Cover the pan and cook over low heat for 6-9 minutes, until the whites are fully set and opaque with no clear jelly left around the yolks, while the yolks still jiggle softly. The lid traps steam that cooks the tops of the whites evenly. For firmer yolks — recommended for pregnant people, young children, older people or anyone more vulnerable to foodborne illness — cook for a further 3-4 minutes until the yolks are fully set and reach 71°C.

  6. Top and Serve

    Scatter the crumbled feta and chopped herbs over the top and bring the pan straight to the table with warm bread for scooping.

Tips

  • If the white nearest the yolk is slow to set, spoon a little of the hot sauce over that spot to help it along without overcooking the yolk.
  • The tomato base can be made up to 2 days ahead; bring it back to a simmer before adding the eggs.
  • Tinned tomatoes vary in acidity from brand to brand, so taste the sauce before the eggs go in and adjust the salt; a few extra minutes of simmering also mellows a sharp batch.
  • Use a pan with a lid, or cover it with a baking tray if you don't have one — trapping the steam is what sets the egg whites on top.

Storage

The tomato sauce keeps well on its own for up to 3 days in the fridge or 3 months in the freezer. Once the eggs are cooked in, shakshuka doesn't reheat well, so it's best eaten freshly made.

Pho Bo: Vietnamese Beef and Rice Noodle Soup

Inspired by techniques from America's Test Kitchen and Serious Eats.

Pho · Classic · Vietnamese

A deeply fragrant beef broth simmered with charred onion, ginger and warm spices, ladled over silky rice noodles with tender shredded shank and thin slices of beef. Fresh herbs, sprouts and lime let everyone finish their own bowl.

Ingredients

Metric · 6 servings

Broth

  • 2 kg bone-in cross-cut beef shank
  • 2 onions, halved (skins on)
  • 75 g fresh ginger, halved lengthwise strained out; not counted
  • 5 garlic cloves, peeled and smashed
  • 10 star anise strained out; not counted
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns strained out; not counted
  • 1 tbsp coriander seeds strained out; not counted
  • ½ cinnamon stick strained out; not counted
  • 5 whole cloves strained out; not counted
  • 4.5 l water

Seasoning

  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 12 g sugar about 1 tbsp
  • 3 g fine sea salt or table salt about ½ tsp

To serve

  • 280 g beef eye of round (topside), in one piece
  • 600 g dried flat rice noodles (banh pho)
  • 20 g fresh coriander, leaves and tender stems
  • 6 spring onions, thinly sliced
  • 50 g onion, very thinly sliced and rinsed
  • 200 g bean sprouts
  • 15 g Thai basil leaves
  • 2 limes, cut into wedges
  • 6 tsp sriracha, to serve (optional) about 1 tsp per bowl; not counted

Method

  1. 1

    Char the aromatics. Heat the grill to high and grill the onion and ginger halves on the foil-lined tray for about 20 minutes, turning, until blackened in patches. Charring adds a sweet, smoky depth that is characteristic of a good pho broth.

  2. 2

    Blanch the shank. Put the shank in the stockpot, cover with cold water, bring to the boil and boil for 10 minutes. Tip into the colander, rinse the meat and bones well and wash out the pot. Blanching removes the blood and impurities that would otherwise cloud the broth.

  3. 3

    Simmer the broth. Return the shank to the pot with the charred onion and ginger, garlic, star anise, peppercorns, coriander seeds, cinnamon, cloves and 4500 ml water. Bring to the boil, then lower the heat so it barely bubbles and simmer for 3 hours, skimming now and then. A slow, gentle simmer extracts flavour and body while keeping the broth clear.

  4. 4

    Freeze and slice beef. Meanwhile, freeze the eye of round on a plate for 35-45 minutes until very firm, then slice it across the grain as thinly as you can, about 3 mm. Cover and refrigerate. Partly freezing the meat makes it firm enough to slice paper-thin, so it cooks in seconds.

  5. 5

    Strain and season. Lift out the shank and shred the meat, discarding the bones and gristle. Let the broth settle for 5 minutes, skim off the fat, then strain it through the muslin-lined sieve into the saucepan; you should have about 3 litres, so top up with water if needed. Stir in the fish sauce, sugar and salt, then taste.

  6. 6

    Prepare the noodles. Soak or cook the rice noodles following the packet instructions until just tender, then drain and divide them among the bowls. Slightly firm noodles are best, as the hot broth will soften them further.

  7. 7

    Cook the sliced beef. Bring the broth to a rolling boil. Lower the sliced beef into it in the small sieve, a few slices at a time, for 30-60 seconds until no pink remains, then lay it over the noodles with the shredded shank. Cooking the slices in batches keeps the broth boiling so every piece cooks through quickly.

  8. 8

    Assemble and serve. Ladle about 500 ml boiling broth into each bowl and top with coriander, spring onions and sliced onion. Serve with the bean sprouts, basil, lime wedges and sriracha for everyone to add at the table.

Cat tip

Use beef and check that the fish sauce contains only fish, salt and sugar.

More from the cat

  • A common mistake is letting the broth boil hard during the long simmer, which emulsifies the fat and makes it cloudy and greasy.
  • Make the broth a day ahead and chill it; the fat sets on top and lifts off easily.
  • Use your sharpest knife on the half-frozen beef for the thinnest slices.

Storage

Cool the strained broth and refrigerate for up to 4 days or freeze for up to 3 months; keep the meat, noodles and herbs separately. Bring the broth to a rolling boil before serving and cook fresh slices of beef in it as above. Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours (within 1 hour on a day above 32°C).

Equipment

  • large stockpot (at least 8 litres)
  • overhead grill (broiler) and foil-lined tray
  • large pot for the noodles
  • colander
  • fine sieve lined with muslin
  • large saucepan (at least 4 litres)
  • spider or large slotted spoon
  • small sieve or wire skimmer
  • sharp knife
  • 6 deep soup bowls

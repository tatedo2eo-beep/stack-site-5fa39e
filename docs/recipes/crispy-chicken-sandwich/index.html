Crispy Buttermilk Chicken Sandwich

Buttermilk-marinated chicken thighs get a double-textured, deeply crunchy crust and a quick fry, then stack into a sandwich with spicy mayo, crisp lettuce and pickles.

Difficulty

Per serving

Ingredients

4 servings

Chicken and marinade

  • 4boneless, skinless chicken thighs about 600 g total, trimmed of excess fat
  • 250 mlbuttermilk
  • 1 tspfine salt
  • 1 tspgarlic powder
  • 1 tspsweet paprika
  • ½ tspcayenne pepper use ¼ tsp for mild

Coating

  • 150 gplain flour
  • 30 gcornflour (cornstarch)
  • 1 tspbaking powder
  • ¾ tspfine salt
  • 1 tspsweet paprika
  • ½ tspground black pepper
  • 1.5 lneutral frying oil (sunflower, rapeseed or vegetable) enough for 5 cm depth

To assemble

  • 4soft burger buns about 60 g each, toasted
  • 60 gmayonnaise
  • 1 tsplemon juice
  • ¼ tspcayenne pepper stirred into the mayonnaise
  • 60 gdill pickle slices
  • 60 gshredded iceberg lettuce

Equipment

  • large bowl and a shallow dish
  • heavy, deep pot or casserole (about 24 cm, at least 10 cm deep)
  • digital probe thermometer (for oil and chicken)
  • wire rack set over a baking tray
  • slotted spoon or tongs

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Cereals containing gluten
  • Eggs
  • Milk
  • Mustard
  • Sulphites

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken

    Whisk the buttermilk with the salt, garlic powder, paprika and cayenne, then add the chicken and turn to coat. Covered and refrigerated for at least 1 hour, or up to 24 hours, the chicken has time to brine: salt helps the meat hold onto its moisture, while the mild acid in the buttermilk softens the surface so the coating has something to grip.

  2. Build the dredge

    Whisk the flour, cornflour, baking powder, salt, paprika and pepper together in a shallow dish. Drizzle in 2 tablespoons of the marinade and rub it through with your fingertips until small shaggy clumps form — these turn into extra-crunchy bits once fried. The cornflour gives a crisper, less greasy crust, and the baking powder adds tiny bubbles that lighten it.

  3. Heat the oil

    Pour oil into the heavy pot to a depth of about 5 cm, keeping it no more than half full so there is room for bubbling. Heat over medium-high until the thermometer reads 175°C; this usually takes 10-15 minutes. Set a wire rack over a tray ready for draining.

  4. Coat the chicken

    Lift each thigh from the marinade, letting the excess drip off, then press it firmly into the flour mixture on both sides, packing the coating on well. Rest the coated pieces on a plate for 5-10 minutes so the coating hydrates slightly — this short rest stops it falling off the moment the chicken hits the oil.

  5. Fry in batches

    Two pieces at a time go into the oil, lowered away from you. The temperature will dip to around 150-160°C; adjust the heat to hold it between 160-170°C. Fry for 6-8 minutes, turning once halfway, until deep golden brown and the thickest part reads 74°C — thighs stay juicy even a little above this.

  6. Drain and rest

    The wire rack, not kitchen paper, is where the chicken should land, since kitchen paper traps steam underneath and softens the crust. Sprinkle with a pinch of salt if you like, and let the oil climb back to 175°C before frying the next batch.

  7. Build the sandwich

    Stir the mayonnaise with the lemon juice and cayenne to make the spicy mayo. Spread it over the toasted buns, then layer on the lettuce, the fried chicken and the pickle slices, and serve immediately while the crust is at its crispest.

Tips

  • Keep an eye on the thermometer: oil that's too cool gives a greasy, pale crust, and oil that's too hot browns the outside before the inside is cooked.
  • Fry only 2 pieces at a time — overcrowding drops the oil temperature and the chicken won't crisp properly.
  • Leave each piece alone for the first minute of frying so the crust has time to set before you move it.
  • Let the oil cool completely before straining and storing it; it can be reused once or twice, but never leave hot oil unattended, and keep a lid nearby in case it ever catches.
  • If the oil ever smokes heavily, turn off the heat; if it catches fire, cover with a lid or damp tea towel and never use water.

Storage

This sandwich is best enjoyed fresh and crisp. Cooked chicken keeps in the fridge for 2 days; reheat on a rack in the oven at 200°C (180°C fan) for 10-12 minutes until piping hot (74°C) and crisp again. Don't freeze the raw chicken while it's still in its buttermilk marinade.

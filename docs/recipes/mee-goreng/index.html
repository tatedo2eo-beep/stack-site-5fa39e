Mee Goreng (Spicy Fried Noodles with Prawns, Tofu and Fried Egg)

Inspired by techniques from NYT Cooking and Serious Eats.

Mee goreng · Classic · Malaysian / Indonesian

Malaysian-style fried egg noodles coated in a smoky chilli sambal and sweet soy sauce, loaded with prawns, tofu and vegetables, and topped with a crisp fried egg.

Prep30 min
Cook25 min
Total55 min
DifficultyMedium

Ingredients

Metric · 4 servings

Noodles

  • 400 g fresh yellow egg noodles (hokkien or lo mein)

Sambal

  • 10 g dried red chillies, seeded, soaked in hot water 15 min
  • 4 shallots, chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 15 g tamarind pulp soaked in 60 ml hot water and strained
  • 12 g light brown sugar about 1 tbsp
  • 3 g fine sea salt or table salt about ½ tsp
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil (rapeseed, sunflower or vegetable)

Sweet soy

  • 2 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 18 g dark brown sugar about 1.5 tbsp; or 3 tbsp ready-made kecap manis

Stir-fry

  • 2 tbsp neutral oil (rapeseed, sunflower or vegetable)
  • 300 g raw peeled prawns
  • 150 g firm tofu, in 1.5 cm cubes
  • 200 g pak choi, chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 150 g bean sprouts

To serve

  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (rapeseed, sunflower or vegetable)
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges

Method

  1. 1

    Blend the chilli paste. Blend the drained soaked chillies, shallots, garlic and 2 tablespoons of water into a smooth paste. Soaking the dried chillies first softens them enough to blend evenly rather than leaving gritty flecks.

  2. 2

    Fry the sambal. Fry the paste in the 3 tablespoons of oil in the wok over medium heat for 8-10 minutes, stirring often, until it darkens, smells fragrant and the oil visibly separates at the edges. Cooking the raw paste this long drives off the harsh, sharp taste of raw shallot and garlic, leaving a deep, rounded flavour.

  3. 3

    Season the sambal. Stir in the tamarind water, sugar and salt and cook for a further 2 minutes, then scrape the sambal into a bowl. The tamarind adds a tartness that balances the chilli's heat.

  4. 4

    Warm the sweet soy. Warm the soy sauce and sugar together in the small saucepan for about 1 minute, just until the sugar dissolves into a glossy syrup. This quick sweet soy mixture is what gives the finished noodles their characteristic dark sheen.

  5. 5

    Loosen the noodles. Loosen the fresh egg noodles in a pot of boiling water for 1 minute, then drain and shake off the excess water. This brief dip separates the strands without cooking them further, since they're already fully cooked.

  6. 6

    Fry tofu and prawns. Wipe out the wok, heat 1 tablespoon of oil over high heat and fry the tofu for 3-4 minutes until golden on the outside, then add the prawns and cook for 1-2 minutes until just opaque, lifting both out once done. Frying the tofu before the prawns go in gives it time to crisp up without the prawns overcooking alongside it.

  7. 7

    Fry noodles with sambal. Add the remaining oil and the sambal to the wok, then add the noodles and toss for 2 minutes to coat them evenly. The hot sambal clings to the strands and starts to colour them as it fries.

  8. 8

    Add pak choi and sweet soy. Add the pak choi and the warm sweet soy mixture and toss for 2 minutes, letting the noodles sit still for a moment between stirs so they catch and darken slightly in places, adding a splash of water if they start to stick. Those caught, slightly charred spots are where the best flavour develops.

  9. 9

    Return tofu and prawns. Add the tomatoes and bean sprouts along with the fried tofu and prawns, and toss for 1 minute until everything is hot and well combined. The tomatoes should just soften without breaking down into the sauce.

  10. 10

    Fry the eggs. While the noodles finish, fry the eggs in the frying pan in 1 tablespoon of oil until the whites are fully set and crisp and lacy at the edges. A crisp edge gives a textural contrast against the soft noodles underneath.

  11. 11

    Serve. Divide the noodles between plates, top each with a fried egg and serve with lime wedges for squeezing over. Eat immediately while the egg yolk is still soft enough to break through the noodles.

Tips

  • Fry the sambal until the oil visibly separates — stopping too early leaves it tasting raw and harsh instead of deep and rounded.
  • Keep the noodles moving once the sambal and sweet soy go in, but let them rest briefly between stirs so they can catch and char slightly for extra flavour.
  • Fry the egg separately rather than cracking it into the noodles; this keeps the white crisp at the edges instead of going soggy in the sauce.
  • Don't skip soaking the dried chillies — unsoaked chillies won't blend smoothly and will leave tough bits in the paste.
  • Add a splash of water if the noodles start sticking to the wok rather than extra oil, which can make the finished dish greasy.

Storage

Store the noodles (without the fried egg) in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days. Reheat in a hot wok or pan with a splash of water until piping hot (74°C) throughout, and fry a fresh egg to serve on top rather than reheating a cooked one. Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours (within 1 hour on a day above 32°C).

Equipment

  • blender or mini food processor
  • small saucepan
  • colander
  • large pot (at least 4 litres)
  • non-stick frying pan
  • wok (35 cm) or large non-stick frying pan (30 cm)
  • wok spatula or tongs
  • small bowls for the prepared ingredients

Smooth Iced Coffee

Brewing coffee double-strength straight onto ice chills it instantly without watering it down, then a splash of cold milk finishes it smooth and balanced. No sugar needed - just good coffee and a little cinnamon if you like.

Ingredients

1 servings
  • 20 gmedium or dark roast ground coffee medium-coarse grind for a cafetière, medium for pour-over
  • 160 mlwater, just off the boil (about 94°C)
  • 100 gice cubes, for flash-chilling
  • 100 gice cubes, for the glass
  • 120 mlcold milk (semi-skimmed) or milk of your choice
  • 1 pinchground cinnamon optional

Equipment

  • kettle
  • cafetière (French press), pour-over cone or espresso machine
  • heatproof jug
  • tall glass (about 400 ml)
  • digital scales

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Milk

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Ice the jug, heat the water

    Put 100g of ice into a heatproof jug, then boil the kettle and let it sit for 30 seconds so the water settles to around 94°C. Water straight off a hard boil is slightly too hot for coffee and tends to pull out harsh, bitter flavours.

  2. Brew double-strength over ice

    Brew directly over the ice in the jug: for a cafetière, combine the coffee with the 160ml of water, stir, steep for 4 minutes, then press and pour over the ice; for a pour-over, pour the water slowly in circles over 2.5-3 minutes directly onto the ice below. If using espresso instead, pull a double shot from the 20 g of coffee straight onto the ice, then add 100 ml cold water. Hot coffee hitting ice chills in seconds and locks in the aroma, while brewing it stronger than usual compensates for the extra water the melting ice will add.

  3. Stir to finish chilling

    Stir the jug until the ice has mostly melted and the coffee feels properly cold to the touch.

  4. Build the glass

    Fill a glass with the remaining 100g of ice, pour the chilled coffee over it, then top with the cold milk and stir. Dust with a pinch of cinnamon if you'd like a little extra warmth to the flavour.

Tips

  • Don't pour hot coffee into a glass with only a little ice - it'll melt too slowly and leave you with a watery, lukewarm drink instead of a properly chilled one.
  • Freeze any leftover brewed coffee into ice cubes - using coffee ice instead of water ice in the glass stops the drink diluting as it melts.
  • Lighter roasts tend to taste fruitier and brighter, while darker roasts lean more chocolatey - pick based on what you enjoy.
  • One glass contains roughly 150-200mg of caffeine depending on the beans and brew; healthy adults are generally advised to stay under 400mg a day, and under 200mg a day if pregnant or breastfeeding, and it isn't suitable for children. Decaf beans work with exactly the same method.
  • No sugar is needed here - the milk's natural sweetness and a pinch of cinnamon do the job instead.

Storage

Brewed coffee without milk keeps covered in the fridge for up to 2 days. Add ice and milk only when you're ready to serve.

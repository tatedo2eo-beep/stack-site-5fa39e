Chicken Tikka Masala · Classic · British-Indian

Signature Chicken Tikka Masala

Charred, yoghurt-marinated chicken simmered in a rich, gently spiced tomato and cream sauce built on deeply browned onions. A homemade take on the beloved curry-house classic.

Ingredients

4 servings

Tikka

  • 750 gboneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into 4 cm pieces
  • 150 gfat-free Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tbsplemon juice
  • 2garlic cloves, finely grated
  • 10 gfresh ginger, finely grated
  • 2 tspgaram masala
  • 1 tspKashmiri chilli powder or 1 tsp paprika plus a pinch of cayenne
  • ½ tspground turmeric
  • ½ tspfine salt

Masala sauce

  • 2 tspneutral oil
  • 15 gunsalted butter
  • 1large onion, finely chopped
  • 3garlic cloves, finely grated
  • 15 gfresh ginger, finely grated
  • 1green chilli, finely chopped deseed for less heat
  • 2 tspgaram masala
  • 2 tspground coriander
  • 1 tspground cumin
  • 1 tspKashmiri chilli powder or sweet paprika
  • 1 tbsptomato purée (paste)
  • 400 gtin chopped tomatoes or passata
  • 150 mlwater
  • ¼ tspfine salt
  • 75 mldouble (heavy) cream
  • 1 tspdried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi), crushed optional but very aromatic
  • 10 gfresh coriander, chopped

Equipment

  • large bowl
  • rimmed baking tray with a wire rack, lined with foil
  • large deep frying pan or casserole (28 cm) with lid
  • stick blender (optional)
  • instant-read thermometer

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Milk

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken

    Mix the yoghurt, lemon juice, garlic, ginger, garam masala, chilli powder, turmeric and salt in a bowl, then stir in the chicken pieces until well coated. Cover and chill for at least 1 hour, ideally 4-24 hours — the yoghurt's mild acidity gently tenderises the surface and helps it char, while the salt seasons the meat right through, not just on the outside.

  2. Char under the grill

    Once marinated, heat the grill (broiler) to high with a shelf about 10 cm from the heat, and spread the chicken pieces on a wire rack over a foil-lined tray so they aren't touching. Grill for 10-12 minutes, turning once, until charred in spots; it doesn't need to be cooked through yet, as it will finish in the sauce. This blistering char is what gives tikka its characteristic smoky flavour, normally produced by a tandoor.

  3. Brown the onion

    Meanwhile, heat the oil and butter in a 28 cm pan over medium heat, then add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 10-12 minutes until deep golden. Don't rush this step: a properly browned onion is what gives the finished sauce its sweetness and body.

  4. Add aromatics and spices

    Stir in the garlic, ginger and green chilli and cook for 1 minute, then add the garam masala, ground coriander, cumin and chilli powder and cook for 30-60 seconds, splashing in a little water if they start to stick and scorch. Finish with the tomato purée, cooking for a further minute to deepen its flavour.

  5. Simmer the sauce

    Pour in the tomatoes, water and salt, then simmer for 10-12 minutes until thick, with the oil starting to separate visibly at the edges — a sign that the water has cooked off and the flavours have concentrated. For a restaurant-style velvety finish, blend the sauce smooth with a stick blender at this point if you like.

  6. Finish and simmer the chicken

    To finish, stir in the cream and crushed fenugreek leaves, then add the chicken along with any juices from the tray. Simmer gently, partly covered, for 8-10 minutes until the chicken is tender and the largest piece reaches 74°C, keeping the heat low so the cream doesn't split. Rest for 5 minutes, scatter over the coriander, and serve with basmati rice or naan.

Tips

  • Thighs stay juicier than breast here, but if you prefer breast it works too — just cook it only to 74°C so it doesn't dry out.
  • Don't skip properly browning the onion; rushing this step leaves the sauce thin and one-dimensional.
  • Kashmiri chilli powder is prized mainly for its deep red colour rather than heat, so it won't make the dish too spicy.
  • If you like more heat, add extra green chilli rather than more chilli powder.
  • Like most curries, this tastes even better the next day once the spices have had time to settle.

Storage

Keep in the fridge for up to 3 days or freeze for up to 3 months. Reheat gently until piping hot throughout (74°C), adding a splash of water if the sauce has thickened.

Egg White and Veg Omelette

A light, protein-packed omelette built mostly from egg whites, filled with pan-cooked vegetables and creamy feta for a satisfying low-fuss meal.

Ingredients

1 servings
  • 180 gegg whites from about 6 large eggs, or from a carton
  • 1large egg
  • 1 tspolive oil
  • 50 gmushrooms, thinly sliced
  • 40 gred pepper, finely diced
  • 30 gbaby spinach
  • 40 gcherry tomatoes, quartered
  • 1spring onion, thinly sliced
  • 20 gfeta, crumbled
  • 1 pinchfine salt optional; the feta is salty
  • 1 pinchground black pepper
  • 1 tspchopped chives or parsley

Equipment

  • non-stick frying pan (20-24 cm) with a lid or a plate to cover
  • heatproof silicone spatula
  • bowl and whisk

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Eggs
  • Milk

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Cook the vegetables

    Heat half the oil in a 20-24 cm non-stick pan over medium-high heat and fry the mushrooms and red pepper for 3-4 minutes, until the mushrooms are browned and any liquid they've released has evaporated. Add the spinach, tomatoes and spring onion and cook for 1 more minute until the spinach wilts down. Tip the vegetables onto a plate - cooking off their moisture now stops the finished omelette turning soggy and watery later.

  2. Whisk the eggs

    Whisk the egg whites, whole egg, pepper and salt (if using) together for about 30 seconds, until evenly combined and slightly frothy on top. The single whole egg isn't essential for setting the omelette, but it adds colour, flavour and a noticeably more tender bite than whites alone. Have everything else ready to go, as this egg mixture cooks fast.

  3. Start the omelette

    Wipe out the pan, add the remaining oil, and set it over medium-low heat for 1 minute before pouring in the egg mixture. Let it sit undisturbed for 20 seconds, then push the set edges towards the centre with a spatula and tilt the pan so the runny egg flows into the gaps, continuing for about 1 minute. Working gently like this builds an even layer rather than a thick, unevenly cooked base.

  4. Cover and finish setting

    Cover the pan with a lid and cook on low for 1.5-2 minutes, until the top looks just set with no glossy wet patches left. Egg whites fully set at around 65°C, but cranking up the heat to rush this only makes them rubbery and squeaky rather than tender. The lid traps gentle heat from above so the top finishes without the base overcooking.

  5. Fill, fold and serve

    Spoon the cooked vegetables and crumbled feta over one half of the omelette, then fold it over with the spatula and slide it onto a plate. Scatter the chopped chives or parsley over the top to finish. Serve it immediately while it's still warm and tender.

Tips

  • Keep the heat low once the eggs go in - whites turn rubbery and squeaky if they're forced to set too quickly on high heat.
  • Make sure the vegetables are properly dried out in the pan before adding them, or the omelette will weep liquid and go soggy.
  • If you separate your own eggs, cover the leftover yolks and keep them in the fridge for up to 2 days for other dishes.
  • Carton egg whites are a convenient, pasteurised shortcut if you don't want to separate eggs yourself.
  • Use a pan close to the stated 20-24 cm size - too large and the omelette spreads thin and dries out.

Storage

This omelette is best eaten straight away while warm. The cooked vegetable filling alone can be made ahead and kept in the fridge for up to 2 days.

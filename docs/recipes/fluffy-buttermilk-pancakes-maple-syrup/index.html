Fluffy Buttermilk Pancakes with Butter and Maple Syrup

Inspired by techniques from NYT Cooking and America's Test Kitchen.

Pancakes (American) · Classic · American

A lumpy buttermilk and soured cream batter, rested briefly, makes tall, tender pancakes cooked on a lightly oiled pan and kept warm in a low oven.

Prep15 min
Cook25 min
Total40 min
DifficultyEasy

Ingredients

Metric · 4 servings

Dry

  • 285 g plain flour
  • 25 g caster sugar
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp bicarbonate of soda
  • 3 g fine sea salt or table salt about ½ tsp

Wet

  • 480 ml buttermilk
  • 60 g soured cream
  • 2 large eggs
  • 45 g unsalted butter, melted and cooled slightly

Cooking

  • 2 tsp neutral oil

To serve

  • 20 g unsalted butter, to serve
  • 120 ml maple syrup, to serve

Method

  1. 1

    Warm the oven. Heat the oven to 100 °C (80 °C fan) and put the wire rack and tray inside. This keeps the first pancakes warm without drying them while you cook the rest.

  2. 2

    Mix the dry ingredients. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, bicarbonate of soda and salt in one bowl so the raising agents are evenly spread.

  3. 3

    Mix the wet ingredients. In the second bowl, whisk the buttermilk, soured cream, eggs and melted butter until smooth.

  4. 4

    Combine and rest. Make a well in the dry ingredients, pour in the wet mixture and fold gently with the spatula until just combined, leaving lumps and a few streaks of flour. Rest for 10 minutes. Overmixing develops the gluten and makes pancakes tough, while resting lets the flour hydrate and the bubbles start to form.

  5. 5

    Heat the pan. Heat 1 tsp oil in the pan over medium heat until shimmering, then wipe it out with kitchen paper to leave only a thin film. Too much oil gives blotchy, greasy pancakes.

  6. 6

    Cook. Pour in 60 ml batter per pancake, 4 at a time. Cook for 2-3 minutes until the edges look set, the base is golden and bubbles on top are starting to burst. Flip and cook for 1-2 minutes more, until golden underneath and the centre springs back when pressed.

  7. 7

    Keep warm. Move the pancakes to the rack in the oven and repeat with the remaining batter, adding a little oil when the pan looks dry. You should get about 16 pancakes, 10 cm across.

  8. 8

    Serve. Stack 4 pancakes on each plate and top with a little butter and the maple syrup.

Tips

  • Flipping too early is the commonest mistake; wait until bubbles break on the surface and the edges look dry.
  • If the first pancake browns before the bubbles appear, turn the heat down a little.
  • Keep the pancakes on a rack, not stacked on a plate, or they steam and turn soggy.
  • Don't press pancakes flat with the spatula, or you'll squeeze out the air.

Storage

Cool, then refrigerate for up to 2 days or freeze, layered with baking paper, for up to 1 month. Reheat in a toaster or a 180 °C oven until hot. Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours (within 1 hour on a day above 32°C).

Equipment

  • whisk
  • rubber spatula
  • 30 cm non-stick frying pan or griddle
  • 60 ml measure or small ladle
  • thin wide spatula
  • wire rack set in a baking tray
  • kitchen paper

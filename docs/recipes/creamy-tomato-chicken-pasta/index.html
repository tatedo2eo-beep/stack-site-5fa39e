Creamy Tomato Chicken Penne

Golden pan-seared chicken simmered in a rich tomato and cream sauce, tossed with penne and wilted spinach for an easy midweek pasta.

Prep
15 min
Cook
25 min
Total
40 min
Difficulty
Easy
Per serving

No added sugar

Ingredients

4 servings

Pasta

  • 320 gdried penne
  • 1 tbspfine salt, for the pasta water in 3 litres of water

Sauce

  • 500 gskinless chicken breast, cut into 2.5 cm pieces
  • ¼ tspfine salt then taste at the end
  • 1 tbspolive oil
  • 1medium onion, finely chopped
  • 3garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • ½ tspdried oregano
  • ¼ tspchilli flakes optional
  • 2 tbsptomato purée (paste)
  • 400 gtin chopped tomatoes
  • 100 mlwater (swilled round the tomato tin)
  • 100 mldouble (heavy) cream
  • 30 gParmesan-style hard cheese, finely grated plus extra to serve if you like (not counted)
  • 80 gbaby spinach
  • 10 gfresh basil leaves, torn

Equipment

  • large pot (at least 4 litres)
  • large, deep frying pan or sauté pan (30 cm) with lid
  • wooden spoon
  • instant-read thermometer

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Cereals containing gluten
  • Milk

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Brown the Chicken

    Season the chicken with the 1/4 teaspoon salt, then heat the oil in the 30 cm deep pan over medium-high heat. Add the chicken in a single layer and brown for 4-5 minutes, turning once, until golden on two sides — it doesn't need to be cooked through yet, since it finishes later in the sauce. Lift onto a plate and set aside.

  2. Soften the Onion

    Add the onion to the same pan over medium heat and cook for 6-8 minutes until soft and translucent, scraping up the browned bits stuck to the base as you go. That fond holds concentrated flavour from the chicken and feeds straight into the sauce. Stir in the garlic, oregano and chilli flakes for 1 minute.

  3. Deepen the Tomato Purée

    Stir in the tomato purée and fry it for 1-2 minutes until it darkens to a brick-red colour. This brief cooking caramelises the purée's natural sugars, softening its raw, tinny edge into something deeper and sweeter.

  4. Simmer the Sauce

    Pour in the chopped tomatoes along with 100 ml water used to rinse out the tin, and simmer uncovered for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it thickens slightly.

  5. Cook the Pasta

    Meanwhile, boil the penne in salted water for 1 minute less than the packet time, then save 150 ml of the pasta water before draining.

  6. Finish the Chicken

    Return the chicken and its juices to the sauce and simmer gently for 4-5 minutes, until the largest piece reaches 74°C in the centre with no pink remaining. Lower the heat, stir in the cream, then take the pan off the boil before stirring in the cheese — adding it while the sauce bubbles hard can make it turn grainy. Stir in the spinach until it wilts.

  7. Toss and Serve

    Add the drained pasta to the pan with a splash of the reserved pasta water, tossing until glossy and the sauce coats every tube. Taste and adjust the salt, then scatter the basil over the top before serving.

Tips

  • Don't let the sauce boil hard once the cream and cheese go in — a gentle simmer keeps it smooth instead of grainy or split.
  • If your pan is on the smaller side, brown the chicken in two batches rather than crowding it, so it colours instead of steaming.
  • Double cream holds up well to simmering; a lighter single cream is more prone to splitting, so stick with double for a reliably silky sauce.
  • Leftover sauce thickens as it sits in the fridge — loosen it with a splash of water as you reheat.

Storage

Keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a pan with a splash of water, or in the microwave stirring occasionally, until piping hot throughout (74°C). The sauce alone freezes well for up to 2 months — just add freshly cooked pasta after thawing.

Malaysian Coconut Rice with Sambal, Crispy Anchovies, Peanuts and Egg

Inspired by techniques from Serious Eats and NYT Cooking.

Nasi lemak · Classic · Malaysian

Fragrant coconut rice is piled high and surrounded by crisp fried anchovies and peanuts, a boiled egg, cool cucumber and a spoonful of chilli sambal for a plate that balances rich, salty, crunchy and fresh in every bite.

Ingredients

Metric · 4 servings

Coconut rice

  • 400 g jasmine rice
  • 400 ml tin full-fat coconut milk
  • 300 ml water
  • 1 lemongrass stalk, white part only, bruised
  • 2 pandan leaves, tied in a knot (optional)
  • 15 g fresh ginger, peeled and thinly sliced
  • 3 g fine sea salt or table salt about ½ tsp

Eggs

  • 4 large eggs

Crispy anchovies and peanuts

  • 60 ml neutral oil
  • 100 g raw red-skinned peanuts (or unsalted peanuts)
  • 40 g dried anchovies (ikan bilis), heads removed
  • 2 g sugar about 0.5 tsp

To serve

  • 60 g sambal oelek (chilli paste); check the label is halal-certified
  • 150 g cucumber, thickly sliced

Method

  1. 1

    Rinse the rice. Tip the jasmine rice into the sieve and rinse under cold running water, rubbing the grains gently between your fingers, 4-5 times until the water runs almost clear. Drain it well before it goes anywhere near the pan. Washing off the surface starch now is what keeps the finished rice light and separate rather than gluey.

  2. 2

    Simmer the aromatics. In the saucepan, bring the coconut milk, water, lemongrass, pandan, ginger and the 0.5 tsp salt to a boil, stirring so nothing catches on the base. Letting these come up to the boil together dissolves the salt and starts drawing the lemongrass and ginger flavour into the coconut milk before the rice is added. A minute or two here is enough; you just want it bubbling.

  3. 3

    Cook the rice covered. Stir in the rice, bring back to the boil, then cover, turn the heat right down to low and cook for 16-20 minutes without lifting the lid. The grains are ready once the liquid is fully absorbed and they feel tender when pressed. Keeping the lid shut traps the steam so the rice cooks evenly from top to bottom instead of drying out on top while the base overcooks.

  4. 4

    Rest, then fluff. Take the pan off the heat and leave it covered for 5 minutes before touching it. Fluff the rice through with a fork and lift out the lemongrass, pandan and ginger. This short rest lets the last of the moisture even out through the grains, so the rice isn't wet at the bottom and dry on top.

  5. 5

    Boil the eggs. While the rice is cooking, lower the eggs gently into the small pan of boiling water and boil for 10 minutes, until the yolks are fully set all the way through. Cool them straight away in the bowl of cold water, then peel and halve. The full 10 minutes is what sets the yolk completely, and the cold-water shock stops the shell sticking to the white.

  6. 6

    Dry the anchovies. Give the dried anchovies a brief rinse in cold water, then pat them completely dry with kitchen paper. This matters more than it sounds: any dampness left on them will spit violently in the hot oil and steam the anchovies instead of letting them crisp.

  7. 7

    Fry the peanuts golden. Heat the oil in the frying pan over medium heat until a peanut dropped in sizzles straight away. Fry the peanuts for 3-4 minutes, stirring often, until they turn from their red skin colour to a deep golden brown, bearing in mind they'll darken a touch more as they cool. Frying gently like this cooks them through to the centre without scorching the outside.

  8. 8

    Crisp the anchovies. Add the anchovies to the same oil and fry for 2-3 minutes, stirring, until they are very crisp and golden. Lift everything out onto the paper-lined plate with the slotted spoon and sprinkle over the sugar, but don't add any salt, since the anchovies are already salty on their own. The hot oil drives off the last moisture fast, which is exactly what gives them their crunch.

  9. 9

    Shape and serve. Press a quarter of the warm rice into the small bowl, then turn it out onto each plate so it holds its dome shape. Arrange the sambal, crispy anchovies, peanuts, egg halves and cucumber around the rice and serve straight away while everything is at its best. The rice is deliberately plain against the punchy toppings, so each forkful is a different balance of salty, crunchy and fresh.

Tips

  • Don't skip drying the anchovies properly: even slightly damp ones spit in the oil and come out chewy rather than crisp.
  • Fry the peanuts before the anchovies in the same oil, as they take longer and the oil is cleaner at that point.
  • Keep the lid on the rice for the full cooking time; lifting it to check lets steam escape and can leave the grains undercooked.
  • Boil the eggs for the full 10 minutes so the yolk is completely set rather than soft in the centre.
  • Taste the sambal before serving, since halal-certified brands vary a lot in heat and saltiness.

Storage

Cool the rice and toppings quickly and refrigerate within 1 hour of cooking, keeping everything for up to 24 hours. Reheat rice only once, until piping hot and 74°C at the centre; the crispy anchovies and peanuts are best eaten fresh rather than reheated. Cool leftovers in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours (within 1 hour on a day above 32°C).

Equipment

  • sieve
  • medium saucepan with a tight lid (about 3 litres)
  • small saucepan
  • bowl of cold water
  • frying pan
  • slotted spoon
  • plate lined with kitchen paper
  • small bowl for shaping

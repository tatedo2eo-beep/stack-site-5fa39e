Flourless Fudgy Brownies

Inspired by techniques from NYT Cooking, America's Test Kitchen and Serious Eats.

Flourless · Classic · International

Deeply chocolatey brownies made without flour, held together by whipped eggs for a fudgy centre and a glossy, crackled top.

Prep20 min
Cook30 min
Total50 min

Ingredients

Metric · 16 servings

Batter

  • 200 g dark chocolate (about 70% cocoa solids), chopped
  • 115 g unsalted butter, cut into pieces
  • 200 g caster sugar
  • 3 large eggs at room temperature
  • 3 g fine sea salt or table salt about ½ tsp
  • 1 tsp alcohol-free vanilla extract
  • 30 g cocoa powder sifted

Method

  1. 1

    Heat oven, line tin. Heat the oven to 175°C (155°C fan) for at least 20 minutes before baking. Line the 20cm metal tin with a baking-paper sling; a metal tin bakes more evenly than glass, which tends to leave these delicate brownies drier at the edges.

  2. 2

    Melt chocolate and butter. Melt the chocolate and butter together in a heatproof bowl set over a pan of barely simmering water, stirring now and then until smooth. Take it off the heat and leave to cool for about 5 minutes so it won't cook the eggs later.

  3. 3

    Whip eggs and sugar. Whisk the eggs, sugar, salt and vanilla on medium-high speed for 5-6 minutes, until pale, thick and roughly doubled in volume, leaving a ribbon trail when you lift the whisk. This whipped foam does the job flour would normally do, trapping air that sets into structure and giving the shiny, crackly top.

  4. 4

    Combine and fold. With the mixer on low, pour in the warm chocolate mixture in a slow stream, then fold in the sifted cocoa powder until no streaks remain. Folding gently keeps as much of that whipped air in the batter as possible.

  5. 5

    Bake. Scrape the batter into the tin, level it, and bake for 25-30 minutes. The top should look set and crackly with a centre that barely wobbles; a skewer should come out with moist crumbs rather than wet batter, and a probe reads around 90-96°C in the middle.

  6. 6

    Cool and chill. Cool completely in the tin, then chill for 1 hour before cutting. Flourless brownies have very little structure to hold them together while warm, so chilling firms them up enough to slice cleanly.

  7. 7

    Cut into squares. Cut into 16 pieces using a hot knife, wiping the blade between cuts for neat edges.

Tips

  • Don't skip the full 5-6 minutes of whipping the eggs and sugar — without flour to fall back on, that whipped volume gives the brownies their structure and crackly top.
  • Let the chocolate mixture cool before folding it in; pouring it in too hot can deflate the whipped eggs and flatten the finished brownies.
  • Chill before cutting rather than slicing while warm — flourless brownies are fragile until the centre firms up in the fridge.
  • Use a metal tin, not glass, for a more even bake; glass retains heat and can dry out the edges before the centre is set.

Storage

Keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days, or freeze the cut squares for up to 3 months. Bring back to room temperature, or warm briefly, before serving.

Equipment

  • 20 cm square metal baking tin (not glass), lined with a baking-paper sling
  • heatproof bowl over a pan of barely simmering water
  • stand mixer or electric hand whisk
  • spatula
  • instant-read thermometer (optional)

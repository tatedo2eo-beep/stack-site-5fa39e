Chocolate Layer Cake

Inspired by techniques from NYT Cooking and Serious Eats.

Chocolate layer · Classic · American

A moist, deeply chocolatey layer cake made with an oil-based batter and coffee for extra depth, finished with glossy chocolate ganache.

Prep30 min
Cook35 min
Total1 h 5 min
DifficultyMedium

Ingredients

Metric · 12 servings

Cake

  • 60 g cocoa powder (Dutch-processed), sifted
  • 240 ml hot strong coffee or boiling water coffee deepens the chocolate; it is not tasted
  • 250 g plain flour
  • 325 g caster sugar
  • 1½ tsp bicarbonate of soda
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 4.5 g fine sea salt or table salt about ¾ tsp
  • 120 g neutral oil (sunflower or rapeseed)
  • 3 large eggs at room temperature
  • 240 g buttermilk, at room temperature
  • 2 tsp vanilla

Ganache frosting

  • 300 g dark chocolate (about 60% cocoa solids), finely chopped
  • 300 ml double cream
  • 60 g unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 g fine sea salt or table salt about ⅛ tsp

Method

  1. 1

    Heat the Oven. Set the oven to 175°C (155°C fan) and let it heat for at least 20 minutes before baking, with a shelf positioned in the middle.

  2. 2

    Bloom the Cocoa. Whisk the cocoa powder into the hot coffee until smooth, then leave it for 5 minutes. Blooming cocoa in hot liquid like this draws out more of its flavour than simply stirring it into the batter dry.

  3. 3

    Mix Wet and Dry Separately. Whisk the flour, sugar, bicarbonate of soda, baking powder and salt together in a large bowl. In a second bowl, whisk the oil, eggs, buttermilk and vanilla, then whisk in the cocoa mixture.

  4. 4

    Combine the Batter. Pour the wet mixture into the dry and whisk just until smooth; the batter will be noticeably thin, which is normal for this style of cake. Overmixing here is the main way to end up with a tough, rubbery crumb instead of a moist one.

  5. 5

    Bake the Layers. Divide the batter between the two tins and bake for 30-35 minutes, until the tops spring back to the touch and a skewer comes out with just a few moist crumbs clinging to it.

  6. 6

    Cool Completely. Cool the cakes in their tins for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and leave to cool fully before icing.

  7. 7

    Make the Ganache. Bring the cream just to the boil and pour it over the chopped chocolate. Leave for 3 minutes, then stir from the centre outwards until glossy and smooth, before stirring in the butter and salt.

  8. 8

    Thicken the Ganache. Leave the ganache at room temperature for 1-1½ hours, stirring occasionally, until it's thick enough to spread, holding its shape rather like soft peanut butter. Whip it for 30 seconds with the mixer if you'd like a lighter, fluffier frosting.

  9. 9

    Assemble. Sandwich the cooled layers with a third of the ganache, then spread the remainder over the top and sides to finish.

Common mistake

Don't skip the coffee bloom - it isn't there for a coffee flavour, just to make the chocolate taste richer.

More from the cat

  • The batter is meant to be thin; resist adding more flour, as it will bake up moist rather than dense.
  • Let the ganache cool and thicken properly before spreading - using it warm will just slide off the sides of the cake.
  • Make sure the cakes are completely cool before assembling, or the ganache will melt and pool.
  • Don't taste the raw dough or batter: raw flour and raw egg aren't safe to eat until baked.

Storage

Keep covered at room temperature for up to 2 days, or in the fridge for up to 5 days, bringing slices back to room temperature before serving. The unfrosted sponges freeze well, wrapped tightly, for up to 2 months.

Equipment

  • two 20 cm round cake tins (5 cm deep), greased and base-lined
  • 2 large bowls and a whisk
  • saucepan
  • stand mixer or electric hand whisk
  • wire racks
  • palette knife

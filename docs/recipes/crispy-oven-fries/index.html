Extra-Crispy Oven Fries

Oven-baked fries with a shatteringly crisp, golden shell and a fluffy centre, thanks to a starch-removing soak and a light cornflour coating. All the crunch of deep-fried chips with far less oil.

Ingredients

4 servings
  • 1 kgfloury or all-rounder potatoes e.g. Russet, Maris Piper or King Edward; peeled or scrubbed
  • 2 tbspneutral oil
  • 1 tbspcornflour (cornstarch)
  • ½ tspgarlic powder
  • ½ tspsweet paprika
  • 2.5 gfine salt (a scant ½ tsp) added after baking; add flaky salt at the table if you like

Equipment

  • 2 large rimmed baking trays
  • baking paper
  • large bowl
  • clean tea towel
  • sharp chef’s knife

Allergens (EU 14)

None of the 14 major EU allergens.

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Preheat the oven

    Heat the oven to 230°C (210°C fan) with two trays inside, positioned in the top and lower third, so they're already hot by the time the fries go in. Starting the fries on a hot tray gives them an immediate sizzle, which helps the outside crisp rather than sitting and softening.

  2. Cut the potatoes

    Cut the potatoes into 1 cm thick batons, keeping them as even in size as you can. Even-sized pieces cook at the same rate, so you won't end up with some fries burnt while others are still soft in the middle.

  3. Soak to remove starch

    Next, place the cut potatoes in a bowl of cold water and leave to soak for 30 minutes (up to 2 hours in the fridge), swishing them around now and then. The water will turn visibly cloudy as surface starch washes away, which stops the fries sticking together and helps them crisp instead of turning gummy.

  4. Dry thoroughly

    Once soaked, tip the potatoes onto a clean tea towel and pat them completely dry. Any water left clinging to the surface will turn to steam in the oven instead of helping the outside crisp, and can also make the oil spit.

  5. Coat with oil and seasoning

    Toss the dried potatoes in a dry bowl with the oil until glossy, then sprinkle over the cornflour, garlic powder and paprika and toss again until no dry powder remains. The thin cornflour coating forms an extra-crunchy shell as it bakes.

  6. Spread on the hot trays

    Carefully pull the hot trays from the oven, line with baking paper, and spread the fries in a single layer with gaps between them across both trays. Crowding the fries traps steam around them, which stops them crisping properly.

  7. Bake and flip

    Bake for 20 minutes without touching them, then flip with a spatula and swap the trays between shelves so they colour evenly. A further 12-18 minutes should bring them to deep golden and blistered at the edges, with a fluffy centre when you pinch one.

  8. Season and serve

    As soon as the fries come out of the oven, sprinkle over the salt while they're still hot — it sticks far better than on cold fries. Serve immediately, with extra flaky salt at the table if you like.

Tips

  • Flipping too early can tear the crust before it's set, so leave the fries undisturbed for the full 20 minutes first.
  • Ovens vary a lot, so start checking for doneness from around 30 minutes total rather than trusting the clock exactly.
  • Thinner-cut fries will cook faster than 1 cm batons, so adjust your timing if you cut them differently.
  • Waxy potatoes stay dense rather than turning fluffy inside — stick to floury or all-rounder varieties like Maris Piper or King Edward.
  • Don't salt the fries before baking; salt draws out moisture and softens the crust instead of crisping it.

Storage

Best eaten hot and fresh. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 2 days; re-crisp in the oven at 220°C (200°C fan) for 8-10 minutes, or in an air fryer at 200°C for 4-5 minutes, until hot through.

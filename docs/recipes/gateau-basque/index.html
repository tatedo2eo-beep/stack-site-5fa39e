Gâteau Basque (Almond Pastry Cake with Custard)

Inspired by techniques from Serious Eats and NYT Cooking.

Basque cake · Classic · French (Basque)

A rustic almond pastry cake from the Basque region, filled with a thick vanilla custard and finished with a scored, golden crust.

Ingredients

Metric · 10 servings

Pastry cream

  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 90 g caster sugar
  • 40 g cornflour
  • 2 tsp alcohol-free vanilla extract
  • 20 g unsalted butter, cold

Pastry

  • 200 g unsalted butter, softened (about 18°C)
  • 180 g caster sugar
  • 3 g fine sea salt or table salt, or table salt about ½ tsp
  • 1 large egg at room temperature
  • 2 large egg yolks
  • 1 unwaxed lemon, finely grated zest
  • 1 tsp alcohol-free vanilla extract
  • 280 g plain flour
  • 80 g ground almonds
  • 1 tsp baking powder

Egg wash

  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 1 tbsp milk

Method

  1. 1

    Make the pastry cream. Heat the milk in a saucepan until steaming. Whisk the egg yolks, sugar and cornflour together, pour in the hot milk while whisking, then return everything to the pan and whisk constantly over medium heat until it thickens and comes to the boil; keep it bubbling for 1 minute. Off the heat, stir in the vanilla and cold butter, then press cling film onto the surface and chill for at least 3 hours. The cornflour lets the custard hold its shape firmly once baked inside the pastry, rather than running out when sliced.

  2. 2

    Make the pastry dough. Beat the butter, sugar and salt together for 2 minutes until just creamy rather than light and fluffy, then beat in the egg, yolks, lemon zest and vanilla. Mix in the flour, ground almonds and baking powder on low speed until it just comes together into a soft dough. Keeping the butter only lightly creamed gives the baked pastry its characteristic short, biscuit-like texture rather than a cakey one.

  3. 3

    Chill the dough. Divide the dough roughly 60:40, shape each piece into a disc, wrap and chill for 2 hours. The dough is very soft at this stage, so it needs this time in the fridge before it can be rolled without sticking or tearing.

  4. 4

    Line the tin. Heat the oven to 175°C (155°C fan) for at least 20 minutes, with a shelf in the lower-middle position. Roll the larger disc between sheets of baking paper to about 30 cm and use it to line the buttered tin, pressing it up the sides; roll the smaller disc to about 24 cm for the lid. Chill both pieces for 15 minutes so the pastry firms back up and holds its shape as the cake is assembled.

  5. 5

    Fill and seal. Whisk the chilled pastry cream until smooth, then spread it into the pastry case, stopping about 1 cm short of the top edge. Lay the pastry lid over the top, press the edges firmly together to seal in the custard, and trim off any excess overhang.

  6. 6

    Finish, bake and cool. Brush the top with egg wash, chill for 10 minutes, then brush again and score a crosshatch pattern into the surface with a fork. Bake for 45-55 minutes until deeply golden all over. Leave the cake to cool completely in the tin, since the custard filling needs this time to firm up before it can be unmoulded cleanly. Serve at room temperature, keeping any leftovers in the fridge.

Tips

  • Chill the dough for the full 2 hours before rolling - it's too soft to handle straight after mixing and will stick to everything.
  • Whisk the pastry cream smooth before spreading it into the case, as any lumps will show once the cake is sliced.
  • Seal the edges firmly when the lid goes on, or the custard can leak out during baking.
  • Let the cake cool fully in the tin before turning out - cutting into it warm means the custard hasn't set enough to hold its shape.
  • Score the crosshatch pattern through the egg wash only, not deep into the dough, so it browns nicely without cutting into the custard underneath.
  • Don't taste the raw dough or batter: raw flour and raw egg aren't safe to eat until baked.

Storage

Once cooled, keep the cake covered in the fridge and eat within 3 days. Serve it at room temperature rather than reheating, since the custard filling is already fully cooked.

Equipment

  • medium saucepan
  • balloon whisk
  • cling film
  • stand mixer or electric hand whisk
  • 23 cm loose-based cake tin or tart ring (4-5 cm deep), buttered
  • rolling pin
  • baking paper
  • fork

Better-Than-Takeaway Chicken Fried Rice

Day-old chilled rice, quick-marinated chicken and just-set egg come together in a hot wok for fried rice with properly separate, toasted grains.

Ingredients

4 servings

Rice

  • 250 glong-grain or jasmine rice, cooked and chilled overnight about 700 g cooked; see step 1

Stir-fry

  • 350 gboneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1.5 cm pieces
  • 1 tspsoy sauce, for the chicken
  • 1 tspcornflour (cornstarch)
  • 3large eggs, beaten with a pinch of salt
  • 2 tbspneutral oil
  • 3garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 10 gfresh ginger, finely chopped
  • 4spring onions, whites and greens sliced separately
  • 300 gfrozen peas and diced carrots no need to thaw

Seasoning

  • 1½ tbspsoy sauce plus extra at the table if you like
  • 1 tsptoasted sesame oil
  • ¼ tspground white pepper

Equipment

  • wok or large frying pan (30 cm), carbon steel or non-stick
  • wide spatula
  • small bowl

Allergens (EU 14)

  • Cereals containing gluten
  • Eggs
  • Soybeans
  • Sesame

Always check the labels of the products you use.

Method

  1. Chill the rice ahead

    The day before, rinse and cook the rice (250 g rice with 375 ml water, simmered covered for 12 minutes, then rested for 10), then spread it on a tray to cool quickly and refrigerate within 1 hour, uncovered until cold and then covered. Chilling firms the starch and dries the surface of the grains, which is exactly what keeps them separate in the wok instead of turning mushy. Break up any clumps with your fingers before you start cooking.

  2. Coat the chicken

    Toss the chicken thigh pieces with the soy sauce and cornflour until evenly coated. This thin, velveting-style coating shields the surface from the fierce heat, so the meat stays juicy, and the starch helps it brown.

  3. Scramble the eggs

    Heat 1/2 tablespoon of oil in the wok over medium-high heat, pour in the beaten eggs, and stir gently for 30-45 seconds until just set into soft curds. Tip them onto a plate — slightly underdone is right here, since they finish cooking when tossed back in later.

  4. Stir-fry the chicken

    Add another 1/2 tablespoon of oil to the wok over high heat, lay the chicken in a single layer and leave it for 1 minute to brown, then stir-fry for 4-5 minutes until cooked through to 74°C. Move it onto the plate with the egg.

  5. Wake up the aromatics

    Add the remaining 1 tablespoon of oil, then the garlic, ginger and spring onion whites, stirring for 30 seconds until fragrant. Tip in the frozen peas and carrots and cook for 2 minutes until hot through.

  6. Toast the rice

    Add the chilled rice, spreading it across the wok and pressing it down, then leave it undisturbed for 30-45 seconds until it toasts and makes a gentle crackling sound before tossing. Repeat this press-and-toss for 3-4 minutes in total, until every grain is hot and separate.

  7. Season and combine

    Drizzle the soy sauce around the hot edge of the wok, where it sizzles and caramelises slightly rather than just soaking in, then add the white pepper. Return the egg and chicken, tossing everything together for 1 minute, and finish off the heat with the sesame oil — stirred in late so its fragrance doesn't cook away — and the spring onion greens.

Tips

  • Freshly cooked, warm rice turns gluey in the wok — if you're short on time, cook it, spread it thinly on a tray and chill it uncovered for at least 1 hour before using.
  • Keep the heat high throughout and don't overload the wok; cook in two batches if your pan is small so everything fries rather than stews.
  • Rice needs careful handling for food safety: cool it quickly, refrigerate within 1 hour, use within 24 hours, and reheat only once until piping hot throughout.
  • Taste before adding extra soy sauce at the end — it's easy to oversalt once everything is already seasoned.

Storage

Cool quickly and refrigerate within 1 hour of cooking, then use within 1 day. Reheat only once, stirring until steaming hot throughout (74°C); freezing isn't recommended.
